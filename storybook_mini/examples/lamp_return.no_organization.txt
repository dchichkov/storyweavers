Once upon a time, Nell lived in a small cottage and wanted to bake a plaited loaf. Ada had a folded invitation. “Iris is leaving tomorrow; a warm loaf might persuade her to stay for supper,” Nell said to Ada. Nell took a wicker basket and a white cloth. “A basket is a hopeful start for a loaf we have not baked,” Ada said. Nell walked with Ada to a stone workshop. Iris stood beside a garden gate and had a brass lamp. “May we borrow your lamp until six; our oven room has no light?” Nell said to Iris. “I need my lamp for my cellar steps,” Iris replied. “Leave one measure of oil for the steps; they are steep enough in daylight,” Iris said. Iris lent the lamp to Nell. “I will return your lamp to you,” Nell said. Nell carried the lamp with Ada to a windowless kitchen. Ada put the cloth on a wooden table. Nell checked a glass reservoir, which was almost empty. Nell examined a brass wheel, which was stiff. “That little wheel has the manners of a locked front door,” Ada said to Nell. Nell turned the wheel. Nell wanted to bake the loaf, but did not light the lamp. “If the lamp stays cold, is this still a surprise supper?” Ada said to Nell. “The last measure belongs to her steps; a loaf cannot make them less steep,” Nell replied. Because Nell closed a brick oven, Nell felt miserable. “Then let us ask about breakfast instead; there is room on the invitation,” Ada said. Nell carried the lamp with Ada to the workshop. “We hoped to give you supper; may we bring a loaf tomorrow, if you are still here?” Nell said to Iris. “Ask me in the morning; the van has not told me its plans either,” Iris replied. Nell returned the lamp to Iris. Iris checked the lamp, which was unlit. Because Ada gave the invitation to Iris, Iris smiled. “I will keep my invitation,” Iris said to Ada. At six, Iris kindled the lamp and walked to the cellar steps. The loaf was unbaked. The lamp shone over the cellar steps.
