A Light at the Landing

full
14 facts · 11 sentences · 3 pronouns · 3 combinations

Once upon a time, Mara lived in a cottage and had a brass lantern. Oren needed the brass lantern at a river landing. Mara lit the brass lantern and carried it to the river landing.

On the riverbank, the wind blew out the brass lantern. As a result, Mara felt afraid.

Oren said, “Cup your hands around the flame.”

Mara shielded the brass lantern and kindled it.

At the landing, Mara hung the brass lantern on an iron hook. As a result, Oren thanked her. Mara felt calm again. As night fell, the brass lantern shone over the river landing.

The Kite with a Blue Patch

full
15 facts · 12 sentences · 3 pronouns · 3 combinations

One windy afternoon, Tomas had a red kite and walked to a grassy hill. He met Ada and launched the red kite.

A moment later, the red kite caught on a low branch. Tomas took down the red kite from the low branch. Because the red kite was torn, Tomas felt miserable.

Ada said, “We can mend it together.”

Ada gave her blue patch to Tomas. Tomas mended the red kite with his blue patch. He thanked Ada.

Before sunset, Tomas sent up the red kite. He felt cheerful again. The blue patch glowed against the evening sky.

Supper Before Six

full
50 facts · 38 sentences · 9 pronouns · 12 combinations

Once upon a time, Nell lived in a small cottage and wanted to bake a plaited loaf. Ada had a folded invitation.

“Iris is leaving tomorrow; a warm loaf might persuade her to stay for supper,” Nell said to Ada. Nell took a wicker basket and a white cloth.

“A basket is a hopeful start for a loaf we have not baked,” Ada said.

Nell walked with Ada to a stone workshop.

Iris stood beside a garden gate and had a brass lamp.

“May we borrow your lamp until six; our oven room has no light?” Nell said to Iris.

“I need my lamp for my cellar steps,” Iris replied. “Leave one measure of oil for the steps; they are steep enough in daylight,” Iris said. Iris lent the lamp to Nell.

“I will return your lamp to you,” Nell said. Nell carried the lamp with Ada to a windowless kitchen.

Ada put the cloth on a wooden table. Nell checked a glass reservoir, which was half full. Nell examined a brass wheel, which was stiff.

“That little wheel has the manners of a locked front door,” Ada said to Nell.

Nell turned the wheel.

Nell lit the lamp and put it on a stone shelf. She put the loaf in a brick oven and baked the loaf.

“It smells like supper already; perhaps we should invite the smell first,” Ada said to Nell.

Before the second baking interval, Iris arrived at the kitchen. Ada opened a kitchen door.

“I need my lamp for my cellar steps,” Iris said to Nell.

“The loaf is for your leaving supper; will you give it ten more minutes?” Nell replied.

“Ten minutes, then; I can finish packing the counter before going downstairs,” Iris replied.

Nell checked the reservoir and baked the loaf. She put the loaf on the table. The loaf was warm. Iris smelled the loaf.

“Now I understand the basket; I thought you were borrowing my whole kitchen,” Iris said to Ada.

Nell blew out the lamp and returned it to Iris. Iris examined the lamp, which was unlit. Because Ada gave the invitation to Iris, Iris smiled.

“I will keep my invitation,” Iris said to Ada.

At six, Iris kindled the lamp and walked to the cellar steps. The loaf was warm. The lamp shone over the cellar steps.

The Measure Left for the Steps

Once upon a time, Nell lived in a small cottage and wanted to bake a plaited loaf. Ada had a folded invitation.

“Iris is leaving tomorrow; a warm loaf might persuade her to stay for supper,” Nell said to Ada. Nell took a wicker basket and a white cloth.

“A basket is a hopeful start for a loaf we have not baked,” Ada said.

Nell walked with Ada to a stone workshop.

Iris stood beside a garden gate and had a brass lamp.

“I need my lamp for my cellar steps,” Iris replied. “Leave one measure of oil for the steps; they are steep enough in daylight,” Iris said. Iris lent the lamp to Nell.

“I will return your lamp to you,” Nell said. Nell carried the lamp with Ada to a windowless kitchen.

Ada put the cloth on a wooden table. Nell checked a glass reservoir, which was almost empty. Nell examined a brass wheel, which was stiff.

“That little wheel has the manners of a locked front door,” Ada said to Nell.

Nell turned the wheel.

Nell wanted to bake the loaf, but did not light the lamp.

“If the lamp stays cold, is this still a surprise supper?” Ada said to Nell.

“The last measure belongs to her steps; a loaf cannot make them less steep,” Nell replied. Because Nell closed a brick oven, Nell felt miserable.

“Then let us ask about breakfast instead; there is room on the invitation,” Ada said.

Nell carried the lamp with Ada to the workshop.

“We hoped to give you supper; may we bring a loaf tomorrow, if you are still here?” Nell said to Iris.

“Ask me in the morning; the van has not told me its plans either,” Iris replied.

Nell returned the lamp to Iris. Iris checked the lamp, which was unlit. Because Ada gave the invitation to Iris, Iris smiled.

“I will keep my invitation,” Iris said to Ada.

At six, Iris kindled the lamp and walked to the cellar steps. The loaf was unbaked. The lamp shone over the cellar steps.

“Iris is leaving tomorrow; a warm loaf might persuade her to stay for supper,” Nell said to Ada. Nell took the wicker basket and the white cloth.

“A basket is a hopeful start for a loaf we have not baked,” Ada said to Nell.

Nell walked with Ada to the stone workshop.

Iris stood beside the garden gate and had the brass lamp.

“May we borrow your lamp until six; our oven room has no light?” Nell said to Iris.

“I need the brass lamp for the cellar steps,” Iris replied to Nell. “Leave one measure of oil for the steps; they are steep enough in daylight,” Iris said to Nell. Iris lent the brass lamp to Nell.

“I will return the brass lamp to you,” Nell said to Iris. Nell carried the brass lamp with Ada to the windowless kitchen.

Ada put the white cloth on the wooden table. Nell checked the glass reservoir, which was almost empty. Nell examined the brass wheel, which was stiff.

“That little wheel has the manners of a locked front door,” Ada said to Nell.

Nell turned the brass wheel.

Nell wanted to bake the plaited loaf, but did not light the brass lamp.

“If the lamp stays cold, is this still a surprise supper?” Ada said to Nell.

“The last measure belongs to her steps; a loaf cannot make them less steep,” Nell replied to Ada. Because Nell closed the brick oven, Nell felt miserable.

“Then let us ask about breakfast instead; there is room on the invitation,” Ada said to Nell.

Nell carried the brass lamp with Ada to the stone workshop.

“We hoped to give you supper; may we bring a loaf tomorrow, if you are still here?” Nell said to Iris.

“Ask me in the morning; the van has not told me its plans either,” Iris replied to Nell.

Nell returned the brass lamp to Iris. Iris checked the brass lamp, which was unlit. Because Ada gave the folded invitation to Iris, Iris smiled.

“I will keep the folded invitation,” Iris said to Ada.

At six, Iris kindled the brass lamp and walked to the cellar steps. The plaited loaf was unbaked. The brass lamp shone over the cellar steps.
