Once upon a time, Nell lived in the small cottage. Nell wanted to bake the plaited loaf. Ada had the folded invitation.

“Iris is leaving tomorrow; a warm loaf might persuade her to stay for supper,” Nell said to Ada. Nell took the wicker basket. Nell took the white cloth.

“A basket is a hopeful start for a loaf we have not baked,” Ada said to Nell.

Nell walked with Ada to the stone workshop.

Iris stood beside the garden gate. Iris had the brass lamp.

“May we borrow your lamp until six; our oven room has no light?” Nell said to Iris.

“I need the brass lamp for the cellar steps,” Iris said to Nell. “Leave one measure of oil for the steps; they are steep enough in daylight,” Iris said to Nell. Iris lent the brass lamp to Nell.

“I will return the brass lamp to you,” Nell said to Iris. Nell carried the brass lamp with Ada to the windowless kitchen.

Ada put the white cloth on the wooden table. Nell checked the glass reservoir. The glass reservoir was half full. Nell checked the brass wheel.

The brass wheel was stiff.

“That little wheel has the manners of a locked front door,” Ada said to Nell.

Nell turned the brass wheel.

Nell lit the brass lamp. Nell put the brass lamp on the stone shelf. Nell put the plaited loaf in the brick oven. Nell baked the plaited loaf.

“It smells like supper already; perhaps we should invite the smell first,” Ada said to Nell.

Before the second baking interval, Iris arrived at the windowless kitchen. Ada opened the kitchen door.

“I need the brass lamp for the cellar steps,” Iris said to Nell.

“The loaf is for your leaving supper; will you give it ten more minutes?” Nell said to Iris.

“Ten minutes, then; I can finish packing the counter before going downstairs,” Iris said to Nell.

Nell checked the glass reservoir. Nell baked the plaited loaf. Nell put the plaited loaf on the wooden table. The plaited loaf was warm.

Iris smelled the plaited loaf.

“Now I understand the basket; I thought you were borrowing my whole kitchen,” Iris said to Ada.

Nell blew out the brass lamp. Nell returned the brass lamp to Iris. Iris checked the brass lamp. The brass lamp was unlit.

Ada gave the folded invitation to Iris. As a result, Iris smiled.

“I will keep the folded invitation,” Iris said to Ada.

At six, Iris lit the brass lamp. Iris walked to the cellar steps. The plaited loaf was warm. The brass lamp shone over the cellar steps.
