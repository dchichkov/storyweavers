The Weight Beneath the Water

By breakfast the mill pond had swallowed the bottom step of the grain store. Ida counted forty sacks through the open door; each represented a family's winter bread. The gate should have released the pond into the empty meadow, but its wheel would not turn. Ren, who had arrived in the valley only last week, stood beside it with wet boots. His bandaged wrist could not lift a heavy load, but he wanted to drag the sacks upstairs before the water reached them.

Osk planted his long iron lever against the wall. 'We never had this trouble before you came,' he told Ren. Ida disliked how easily the words crossed the yard. She asked to borrow the lever and Osk handed it over, though it remained his tool. Ren took his hands from the wheel. With no one touching it, water continued to press against the gate; the accusation had changed the company in the yard, but had changed nothing beneath the pond.

Ida opened the maintenance diagram beside the wheel. Its two drawings showed a small upward lift before a brace could slide beneath the gate's weight. She followed both lines with her finger, shutting out the calls from the store. By the time she closed the cover, she could repeat both steps without looking.

Before risking the heavy gate, Ida knelt at the little sluice that fed the mill's washing trough. She lifted its jammed plate a finger's width, then slipped a stone underneath. The plate came free and the trough filled. Ren watched the stone hold it up. This was a modest success, but now the marks on the page had become a movement Ida's hands could recognize.

Ren lay flat on the walkway and held Ida's sleeve while she reached beneath the gate housing. She brought up a bent retaining pin, still fixed at one end, with a bright scrape where it caught the runner. Osk bent down to look. 'That was worn before he arrived,' Ida said. Osk withdrew his accusation aloud. Ren was cleared, but the bent pin still caught the runner. Their new agreement had not repaired the iron.

Ida placed Osk's lever beneath the runner and repeated the lift she had practiced. The heavy runner began to rise, then dragged her arms down. Osk refused to hold it for her. She lowered it carefully rather than trap her hand under the weight. Knowing the sequence had brought her to the right place, but she could not be both lifter and brace at once. The gate stayed shut.

By evening they had carried twenty-four dry sacks upstairs. Sixteen lay spoiled below, their seams dark in the water. Ida returned Osk's lever and set a chalk mark where the bent pin needed repair. Ren sat with the others dividing the saved grain; no one blamed him now, but no apology could replace the lost bread. Below their feet, the flooded bottom step disappeared whenever the water stirred. The gate would need another attempt before morning.
