By morning the fern had lifted its leaves. Rae crouched beside it again. "There you are." The pot was still small. The space its leaves made above it was not.

## greenhouse:1002

{"method": "wait", "outcome": "one_waits", "purpose": "light"}

The sunny sill had room for only one pot. Rae's fern needed two hours of bright light, and Ivo's ivy needed the same. Putting a larger wish in the window would not make the sill wider.

Rae put the fern on the sill for its two-hour turn. When its time was over, Rae lifted it down and set it back on the bench. "Finished does not mean yours forever," Rae told the fern. The bright place was empty again.

Ivo came in from the porch with the ivy after the fern's turn had ended. There was less than a full two-hour turn of daylight left. "I am late," Ivo said. "The sun noticed," Rae said, softly.

The growing lamp had no charge left. Rae put the ivy on the sill, but could not give it the missing full turn. Ivo stood beside the pot. "I wanted the day to wait for me." Rae left the fern on the bench. At least the ivy would keep the place by the glass.

The greenhouse grew quiet overnight. The fern stayed on the bench; the ivy had the place on the sill. They were no longer competing for the same square of wood.

By morning the fern was ready, but the ivy still needed its proper light turn. Its pot waited at the glass. Ivo looked at the waiting plant, not at the clock this time.

Q: Where did the plants spend the night?

A: No. The fern had enough light, but the ivy still needed its full turn.

The fern needed water. Rae had a can and a tap, which seemed like all the things a watering morning ought to need.

Rae filled the can at the tap. Before the water reached the fern, it ran out through a leak and made a puddle. Ivo looked at the floor. "That is not the plant." "I had noticed," Rae said.

Rae looked at the empty can beside the new puddle. Filling it again without changing anything would only make a larger puddle. "The fern is not drinking it," Rae said. "The can is losing it."

There was a spare washer. Rae fitted it to stop the leak, then filled the can again. This time the water stayed inside. "Much better manners," Ivo told the can.

Rae checked the fern's soil. It was dry. The water from the repaired can went into the pot this time, not onto the floor. Ivo watched the puddle stay exactly the same size.

They left the watered fern overnight. The puddle did not need another drink, and the pot did not need another whole canful.

Morning found the fern recovered and the mended can still sound. Rae looked at the leaves, then at the dry outside of the can. Two things had been helped by finding one small hole.
