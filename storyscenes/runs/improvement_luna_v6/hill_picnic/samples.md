## The Picnic That Came to You — seed 1000

At the hill foot, Moss stood beside an empty wagon, certain he had been hired as a hauling machine. Tomas stared uphill, certain Aunt Juniper would soon arrive there. Lila looked between them. “Before anyone trots anywhere,” she said, “we need to find out what is actually happening.”

She glanced toward the path and noticed exactly where Aunt Juniper was waiting: at the hill foot, beside the wagon’s wheel.

Moss agreed to pull one bundle. Tomas held the wagon tongue while Moss gave a proud little snort. “Not a machine,” Lila said. “A very important parade donkey.”

“Tomas,” Lila said, pointing downhill, “Aunt Juniper is right here at the foot.” His hilltop plan wobbled like jelly. Then he looked at the waiting wagon and began thinking of a new kind of grand.

Lila lifted the picnic basket into the wagon. The wagon settled with a friendly creak, as if it had just remembered the smell of sandwiches.

Lila tied the brass bell to Moss’s harness. He gave one testing step, and the bell answered, ding!

Moss led the one-bundle procession to Juniper, bell chiming at his shoulder. He stopped beside her and lifted his nose, waiting. Tomas began clapping first.

The little procession ended at Juniper’s feet. Moss stood shining with donkey pride, the bell resting against his harness, waiting—and waiting—until Aunt Juniper applauded with both hands.

Lila found the wagon already stuffed with two picnic bundles. “Food and decorations,” she said. Far above, the brass bell waited on the hilltop. Aunt Juniper could climb, and she wanted the hill picnic, but something would have to make the journey first.

Lila watched Aunt Juniper’s careful step and discovered the important truth: Juniper could climb the hill. “Then the hilltop is not just a dream,” Lila whispered.

She glanced toward the path and noticed exactly where Aunt Juniper was waiting: at the hill foot, beside the wagon’s wheel.

Lila listened and discovered what Juniper wanted: a hill picnic, not a grand fuss. “Aha,” she said. “We can make the hill welcome her.”

“Aunt Juniper, will you come to the hilltop?” Lila invited. Juniper smiled, tested her knee, and nodded. The hill path suddenly seemed less like a climb and more like a long green ribbon waiting to be followed.

“Tomas,” Lila said, pointing downhill, “Aunt Juniper is right here at the foot.” His hilltop plan wobbled like jelly. Then he looked at the waiting wagon and began thinking of a new kind of grand.

Juniper climbed slowly, with Lila on one side and Tomas on the other. At the top she turned around. “I have conquered a hill,” she announced. “It was wearing grass.”

At the hilltop, Tomas rang the brass bell for Juniper. Its bright notes skipped down the green slope, over the wagon, and into the empty picnic plates below.

Lila poured berry fizz into three cups, and Aunt Juniper’s smile rose with every purple bubble.

At the hilltop, Aunt Juniper’s empty plates caught the last bell notes as they tumbled down the sunny slope. Below, the wagon waited with its decorations, ready for the next impossible picnic.
