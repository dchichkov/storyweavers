## The Museum of Small Things — seed 1000

Ada planned a display of dull little things—until the box promised a surprise. The invitation was ready, Ben was polishing the welcome sign, and Pip watched the lid with one bright eye. Somewhere inside waited the blue button Pip called his prize. He had not yet decided whether prizes could be shared.

Ada peeked inside and gasped. “I found Pip’s blue button!” The button flashed like a tiny puddle of sky, and Pip clapped both wings over it.

Ada handed Ben the invitation. He tucked it safely under his elbow and hurried to welcome the library keeper. Ben greeted the keeper at the shed door. “Come in,” he whispered, “unless your shoes are secretly elephants.”

Ben watched the keeper glance at the quiet shelves. “She expects a silent museum,” he noticed. Even Pip’s next feather-flick seemed to make a question mark.

Ben hurried to Ada and shared the news: the keeper hoped for a quiet collection.

Ada opened the museum box with a flourish. “Behold!” she said. The lid creaked, the sunlight hopped in, and every small secret inside became part of the morning.

Pip stared at the blue button, then at Ada’s brass key. “A trade,” he chirped. The button became the museum’s sparkle, while the key found a new perch beneath Pip’s pleased beak.

Ada set the blue button beside the brass key. Sunlight bounced from one to the other, and Pip bowed so deeply that his tail feathers tickled the floor.

Ada led a whisper-tour past the little wonders. She breathed the marble’s story, the button’s sparkle, and the key’s journey. The keeper leaned close, smiling as softly as dust settling on a windowsill.

The keeper’s smile widened until it seemed to have room for every small thing in the shed.

Ada’s quiet tour ended in a hush full of wonder. Pip’s feathers trembled with unplayed music, and the keeper’s delighted smile floated above the display like a warm little moon.

## The Museum of Small Things — seed 1001

Ada held up the invitation card. “Today our museum opens!” she announced. The wooden box was meant to hold the red marble, but Ben knew that little treasure had come from his sister. Pip puffed his feathers. “A museum may borrow,” he said, “but it must ask nicely.”

Ada opened the museum box with a flourish. “Behold!” she said. The lid creaked, the sunlight hopped in, and every small secret inside became part of the morning.

Ada handed Ben the invitation. He tucked it safely under his elbow and hurried to welcome the library keeper. Ben greeted the keeper at the shed door. “Come in,” he whispered, “unless your shoes are secretly elephants.”

Ben touched the marble but did not pocket it. “It belongs to my sister,” he said. “I’ll ask it to lend us its best shine.” Ada made room for a little note: ON LOAN, WITH A HUG.

Ben watched the keeper glance at the quiet shelves. “She expects a silent museum,” he noticed. Even Pip’s next feather-flick seemed to make a question mark.

Ben hurried to Ada and shared the news: the keeper hoped for a quiet collection.

Ada and Ben arranged the marble beside its note. “Borrowed, not captured,” Ben said. The tiny red globe gleamed proudly, as if it knew its owner might come visiting.

Ada led a whisper-tour past the little wonders. She breathed the marble’s story, the button’s sparkle, and the key’s journey. The keeper leaned close, smiling as softly as dust settling on a windowsill.

The keeper’s smile widened until it seemed to have room for every small thing in the shed.

The trusted display held its little loan note, with room for the red marble’s story. The keeper smiled at the careful words, and Ben’s grin shone brighter than a button in the sun.
