## The Roof-Post Invitation Flight — seed 1000

Mina set the neat invitation stack beside the red post. Pip bounced on his toes. “I can carry, fly, or make it fancy!” he announced. The rooftop bell was still quiet, but the moonberry party was already tugging everyone in three different directions.

Mina slid the stack beside the post and tied her promise to it like an invisible bow. “Before the bell,” she said. Pip nodded so hard that a tiny smoke puff escaped his nose.

Mina peered at the crowded hooks and discovered that only the hook marked “bird-high” was open. Jun gave a pleased coo from above.

Mina fanned the stack and discovered sturdy cards, cheerful and firm. “These can survive a Pip-sized adventure,” she said, though she still reached for a ribbon.

With the post’s open hook in mind, Mina chose its little numbered perch. With the post’s crowded hooks in mind, Mina chose a snug perch among them for the ribboned invitations.

Mina carried the ribboned invitations to the chosen hook and posted them neatly. The cards settled against the red post, ready to call the neighbors to moonberries and dancing.

The moonberry invitation rested on its chosen hook, bright against the red post. Mina rang the rooftop bell, and somewhere beyond the chimney, a neighbor answered with an excited whoop.

## The Roof-Post Invitation Flight — seed 1002

Mina placed the invitations by the roof post and promised, “Neighbors first, berry feast second!” Pip wiggled his wings, eager to help. Above them, Jun tilted his head toward the quietest corner of the garden, where a pigeon-sized party might be just right.

Mina slid the stack beside the post and tied her promise to it like an invisible bow. “Before the bell,” she said. Pip nodded so hard that a tiny smoke puff escaped his nose.

Mina looked up. “Jun, where should the bird guests gather?” Jun fluttered to the quiet corner and tapped the tiles twice. Mina smiled. “A quiet corner it is.”

Mina lifted the top card and discovered delicate paper, soft as a moth wing. She quickly lowered it. “This stack needs gentle feet, not grabby dragon claws.”

With the post’s crowded hooks in mind, Mina chose a snug perch among them for the ribboned invitations. Mina told Pip about Jun’s quiet corner, and Pip’s round eyes grew rounder still.

Pip studied the quiet-corner plan, then brightened. “I can decorate one invitation for Jun and fly another for the neighbors!” He offered the paper sail, not his claws. Mina blinked, then began tying the ribbon.

Mina listened to Pip’s plan and nodded. “One card for Jun’s quiet corner, one for the moonberry crowd.” Pip grinned, and the ribbon between them looked suddenly like a tiny bridge.

Jun swooped ahead, marking the quiet route with three silver wingbeats.

Jun guided them to the quiet corner, where the shared invitations arrived beside the berry basket. One card wore a dragon-drawn star; another rested under Jun’s careful wing.

Jun guided a bird-sized invitation into the quiet corner while Pip delivered the party card. Around the berry basket gathered three kinds of footsteps: Mina’s, Pip’s dragon-toes, and Jun’s quick little wingbeats.
