## The Courtyard Wind-Up Show — seed 1000

The red curtain hung neatly, but Nia was hiding behind the low stool. “I want to perform,” she whispered to Mara, “only if the audience watches my shadows, not my nose.” Mara smiled. The blue ribbon lay in its basket, bright as a tiny river. “Then we’ll make a secret-sized show.”

Mara clapped twice, and the courtyard audience gathered with pockets full of crumbs and curiosity. Nia peeked out. Bo bowed so deeply his hair brushed the stage.

Nia leaned close and told Mara exactly what she wanted: a shadow role behind the curtain, where her paper hands could dance without every eye landing on her face. “My nose is not ready for fame,” she whispered.

Nia swallowed, then nodded. “I can make one small shadow.” Mara squeezed her hand. “Small shadows can have enormous ears.” Nia stepped toward the curtain, braver by exactly one bright button.

Mara placed the blue ribbon in Nia’s hands. “For your secret screen,” she said. Nia held it carefully, as if it were a doorway only she could open.

Nia and Mara folded the curtain into a shadow screen. Behind it, their fingers became rabbits, clouds, and one very suspicious potato. Nia laughed softly. The screen glowed, ready for a story no staring face could spoil.

Behind the glowing screen, Nia’s paper hands discovered a moon, a shy rabbit, and a potato who wanted a hat. The audience leaned close and stayed wonderfully quiet. Nia’s little shadow bowed before her face ever needed to.

The courtyard stayed hushed for one soft breath after Nia’s final shadow bowed. Then everyone smiled, including Nia. Around the low stool, the audience made a moon of themselves, and her paper hands rested happily inside it.

## The Courtyard Wind-Up Show — seed 1001

A breeze had knotted the red curtain into a lumpy red octopus. Mara blinked at it. “Our show has grown elbows!” Bo reached for the blue ribbon, while Nia grinned from behind the stage. Puck waited in his basket, ready for whatever ridiculous rescue might happen next.

Mara clapped twice, and the courtyard audience gathered with pockets full of crumbs and curiosity. Nia peeked out. Bo bowed so deeply his hair brushed the stage.

Mara tugged one corner of the curtain and discovered a breeze-tangled knot, with Puck caught in its folds like a kite waiting for a sneeze. “Nobody panic,” she said. “Except perhaps the octopus.”

The ribbon changed hands, from Mara to Bo, and became a bright blue promise against the red tangle.

Bo pulled gently. The ribbon caught the curtain, the curtain released Puck, and Puck swooped upward with a flap-flap-flap. “A rescue!” cried Mara. “A kite!” cried Bo. The knot, at least, had nothing left to say.

Bo and Mara sent Puck sailing above the curtain. The blue ribbon hummed, Puck dipped, and Bo caught him with a triumphant “Ta-da!” Nia laughed from the wings as the audience clapped like a flock of tiny wings.

The blue ribbon hummed, Puck swooped, and the applause fluttered up to meet him. When the children bowed, the red curtain looked less like a problem and more like the doorway to a very excellent breeze.

## The Courtyard Wind-Up Show — seed 1002

The curtain was tidy, Nia was ready, and Bo had a very large idea. He waved at the empty courtyard as if it were packed with people. “My grand finale begins with a parade!” Mara whispered, “A parade needs feet.” Bo tied on his biggest smile and went to find some.

Mara clapped twice, and the courtyard audience gathered with pockets full of crumbs and curiosity. Nia peeked out. Bo bowed so deeply his hair brushed the stage.

Mara discovered the important missing thing: nobody had invited the courtyard audience to follow the show. The path was empty except for one beetle. “Even the beetle needs a proper invitation,” she said.

Mara found Bo’s plan beneath the paper sun: a grand finale with a parade, a fanfare, and “possibly one moon.” She looked toward the uninvited courtyard. Bo whispered, “The audience will want enormous.”

Mara told Bo the audience was not close yet. Bo tapped his chin. His parade plan suddenly needed legs, invitations, and perhaps fewer trumpets.

Bo marched to the courtyard edge and called, “Follow the paper sun!” The audience came closer, then closer still, until the stage had feet all around it. Bo’s grand plan suddenly had exactly the number of spectators it needed.

Bo lifted the paper sun high above the stage. It wobbled magnificently, like a pancake escaping breakfast. Mara set the cloud puppets bobbing low. They drifted around Bo’s knees, soft and lumpy as traveling sheep.

Bo led the weather parade beneath the paper sun, with cloud puppets bobbing behind him and Mara marching beside a giggling audience. Nia carried a cloud that sneezed confetti. The grand finale had become gloriously, wonderfully mobile.

Bo marched under the paper sun with clouds bobbing behind him, while Mara led the cheerful crowd across the courtyard. The once-serious stage stood empty and pleased, as if it had grown a pair of dancing feet.
