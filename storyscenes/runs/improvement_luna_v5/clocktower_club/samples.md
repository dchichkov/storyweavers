## The Clocktower Club: Three Chimes and a Dragon — seed 1000

Ada found the feather flag raised in the clocktower window, but the neighbor below was asleep. “Our noisy first meeting may wake the whole staircase,” she whispered. Pip tucked his wings around his sneeze. The new club had an invitation, a bell, and one very drowsy audience member.

Ada leaned beneath the dusty gears and discovered that the feather flag was blue. “Blue means mystery!” she said, while its tail tickled her ear.

Pip tugged the short rope beside the bell and discovered it was the soft practice rope: the brass bell gave a little spoon-in-a-teacup ting. “That is not a roar,” he breathed. “That is a very polite ding.”

Mr. Bellweather tapped the flagpole. “That signal means the tower is being aired out, not that a club guest has arrived.” Ada’s eyebrows hopped upward. “So the ribbon must do the inviting!”

Ada passed the flag’s true meaning to Pip, who stopped preparing his most enormous entrance sneeze. Once Pip understood the flag was no invitation, Ada handed Mr. Bellweather the red ribbon, its two tails swishing like tiny flags.

Mr. Bellweather carried the ribbon to the tower door and delivered the invitation. “Clocktower Club begins now,” he declared. Ada opened her mouth to cheer, then remembered the sleeping staircase and made a silent cheer instead.

Ada opened the club notebook on a crate. Pip drew a dragon wearing a bell for a hat, and Mr. Bellweather set a dusty gear beside the page as the club’s first meeting table.

With the notebook ready, Mr. Bellweather placed the wooden key in Pip’s warm claw. “Keeper of the beginning,” he said.

Pip turned the key. The bell’s latch clicked open, revealing two ropes: one thick and grand, one short and soft. Pip stared at the soft rope as if it had just winked.

Pip chose the short rope. “A tiny chime can still be brave,” he said. Ada squeezed his shoulder, and his sneeze hid behind a gear until it felt ready to behave.

The three club members performed their first meeting around the crate. Pip pulled the soft rope, and the bell gave one golden blink—small, clear, and proud enough to make the dusty gears listen.

The neighbor stayed asleep, but the quiet meeting stirred no eyelids. Ada, Pip, and Mr. Bellweather leaned together in a whispering circle while the tiny bell sound curled gently up the stairs.

The new club’s name curled across the notebook page. Beside it, the sleeping neighbor breathed on, while Pip’s gentle bell gave the tower one small golden blink and Ada’s ribbon tails settled like red smiles.
