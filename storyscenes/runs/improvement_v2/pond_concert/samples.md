## The Moonlit Pond Concert — seed 1000

The floating stage bobbed beside the bank while Mira planned glittering cheers, Tom polished his drumsticks, and Pip whispered, “A quiet song for Nella.” Nobody yet knew whether Nella was behind the cattails or away.

Pip pressed one toe to the mud and listened. Behind the cattails, he discovered that Nella’s hearing was keen. “Then even a whisper can be a concert,” he said.

“I want to play the moon-song for Nella,” Pip told Tom. “It has three soft notes and one enormous feeling.” Tom looked at the drum, which suddenly seemed very enormous too.

Pip leaned toward Tom. “Nella can hear keenly,” he whispered. Tom’s drumstick froze halfway to the sky. “Keenly?”

Mira and Pip pushed the reed stage onto the pond. It floated proudly, though one corner wiggled like a ticklish duck. Pip sent the paper invitation sliding through the cattails, where it landed with a polite little plop.

Tom practiced a clever ripple: tap-tap, rumble, BOOM. “It begins like a raindrop,” he announced, “and ends like a hippo sneezing.”

A hand appeared between the cattails and accepted the invitation. Nella did not step out yet, but one bright eye peeked toward the pond.

Tom looked at Pip, then at the drum. “All right,” he said. “Your moon-song first. I’ll make my finale small enough to fit in a frog’s pocket.” Pip beamed; Tom’s drumstick gave a brave little salute.

Mira draped the lantern ribbon across the stage. The lights blinked on, making the pond look sprinkled with tiny moons. The invitation fluttered, the lanterns glowed, and Mira called, “Welcome to the pond concert!” From the cattails came a pleased rustle.

Tom struck the finale. BOOM-BOOM-BOOM! Frogs cheered, lily pads bounced, and Tom grinned so widely that his ears nearly became parentheses.

The frogs presented Tom with the Golden Tadpole Spoon. He lifted it high; it wobbled in the moonlight like a very tiny trophy moon.

The drum’s last boom rolled away, and Tom tucked the wobbling spoon beside his heart.

The last drumbeat bounced off the lily pads, and Tom’s Golden Tadpole Spoon flashed brighter than the fireflies.
