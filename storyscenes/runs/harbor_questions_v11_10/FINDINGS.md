# Recovery Paths And The Judge Calibration Problem

## Intervention

v11 adds two enacted continuations, preserving all earlier batches:

- Remove a physical cover before acquiring fresh inspection evidence. The
  original unknown observation remains unknown. A reported second inspection
  needs a new report, hearing and comprehension before Ari changes the plan.
- After Cy declines a heavy lift to keep an earlier promise, Ari can move two
  slabs separately and actually repack the crate on the cart. Moving an empty
  crate does not satisfy this outcome; Cy's declined request is not fulfilled.

These are bounded authored policies over the existing capability laws, not a
general planner. Beliefs and goals remain attached to physical actors and
objects; changed state, not prose assertions, permits the continuation.

## Human Review Overrides A Passing Average

The user found the displayed stories substantially worse than storyscripts.
That is an acceptance failure, not something to dismiss because judges gave
seven. The randomly displayed v11 story was `harbor_questions:63041`, from the
**unjudged hundred**, not a story individually awarded 7.6 or 7.1. The earlier
displayed empty-crate story came from v9. Do not transfer ten-story averages
to either context.

Concrete defects in 63041:

- `instructional_voice`: "The second trip was as real as the first" explains
  bookkeeping rather than developing a scene. Several paragraphs interpret
  a simple action immediately after narrating it.
- `weak_turn`: discovering paint repairs a mistaken delivery, but the original
  need for cups remains unresolved. Moving the paint gives a physical endpoint,
  not necessarily a satisfying conclusion to the opening promise.
- `unintegrated_subplot`: the key-return thread supplies a second deadline but
  little felt consequence or characterization here. The sentence covering
  work being finished, shared or left for later reads like generic template
  commentary instead of this particular story.
- `abstract_child_voice`: the narrator talks about arrangements and whether
  facts count as completion more than the characters' lived experience.

These are literary defects even when every physical transition is valid.
The earlier displayed `harbor_questions:63024` has the same problem in a shorter
form: it never explains why Ari needs the stones, then calls the empty crate
useful without giving it a concrete use. "The contents and the first attempt
now belonged to the same explanation" is reasoning commentary, not a child's
scene. Cy has already explained the discovery when Ari asks to be told what
they have, making the exchange feel assembled out of order. A change of object
location does not by itself earn the claimed satisfying payoff.

The historical judge is poorly calibrated for this distinction: its sole
anchor is a simple race story rated 7 for storytelling and overall, its scale
anchors only 0 (disaster) and 9 (good), and its storytelling checklist stresses
premise, state-driven turn and concrete ending. The returned evidence notes
mostly reward those components. This is a plausible explanation for generous
scores, not a demonstrated causal attribution to any single prompt sentence.

## Next Gate

Do not promote v11 or launch another broad optimization trial on these scores.
First build a separate, versioned literary calibration set with exact saved
storyscripts/StoryWorld examples, these criticized outputs, readable-but-dull
controls and stronger stories. Preserve the current rubric and raw scores.
Use blind pairwise preference plus explicit judgments of story logic,
motivation, dialogue, narrative voice and payoff; include user-ranked anchors
and held-out cases. Verify that it ranks genuinely better prose above
mechanically complete event commentary before optimizing against it. Do not
merely demand lower numbers, or add more scene furniture to pass a checklist.

The full goal remains incomplete: earlier replication established reasonable
judge scores across three scripts, but not convincing prose and diversity.
