## The Pond Concert and the Listener in the Reeds — seed 1000

The concert began with one puzzle: a little shell chime had arrived from the reeds, and Jojo mistook it for a prize token. Nella’s invitation had already been delivered, but her answer had not. Pip tapped the lily pad. “A mystery audience!” he croaked. Mina smiled. “Then let’s play kindly.”

Jojo pointed toward the pond. “Give us thunder!” Pip puffed his cheeks until he looked like a green balloon. With Nella still away, he chose the splashy plan. “Then thunder shall have warts,” he declared, and the tadpoles cheered before the concert even began.

Mina hung the reed bells around the lily-pad stage, where they trembled whenever Pip cleared his throat.

Pip blasted a booming croak across the pond. The bells rattled, the tadpoles cheered, and Jojo leaped so high he nearly applauded a dragonfly. The Golden Pebble landed in his hands at last. Beside the stage, the microphone bobbed like a silver duck enjoying every ridiculous note.

The pond erupted in cheers for Pip’s booming finale, and Jojo hugged the Golden Pebble to his chest. Farther out, the floating microphone bobbed on its cord like a silver duck. Even the ripples seemed to be laughing as the ridiculous prize flashed in the dusk.

## The Pond Concert and the Listener in the Reeds — seed 1001

At sunset, Pip polished his frog-sized microphone while Jojo guarded it like a treasure. Nella was somewhere in the reeds, and Jojo was sure she disliked music. Mina frowned at the empty shore. “Before Pip booms,” she said, “we should find out who is listening.”

Jojo handed Pip the floating pebble microphone. “Use it wisely,” he said. Pip held it to his throat. “I shall use it croakfully.” The pebble bobbed between them like a silver duck waiting for its first song.

Mina followed the faint shell-click to the reed nook. There she discovered exactly where Nella was listening: tucked among the green stems, close enough to hear every splash. “Aha,” Mina whispered. “Our audience has a hiding place.”

Mina listened again and discovered something better than a guess: Nella liked music. The snail simply preferred songs that did not leap out like a startled frog. Mina tucked that discovery into her smile and hurried back to the others.

Mina hung the reed bells around the lily-pad stage, where they trembled whenever Pip cleared his throat.

Mina carried the paper invitation to the reeds. “Nella, the lily-pad stage is ready,” she called. “You may come close, or listen from your favorite wrinkle in the grass.” The invitation slipped between the stems, and a shy shell gave one hopeful tick.

Nella emerged from the reeds, carrying the invitation on her shell. Mina made room beside the lily pad. “Welcome!” Jojo said, much less loudly than usual. Nella settled near the stage, and Pip lowered his microphone. The audience had arrived—and it had brought its own small hello.

Nella offered her shell chime, but Pip offered something glitterier: the Golden Pebble. “For your music?” he asked. Nella accepted. Jojo stared as his prize rolled gently into the reeds, while Pip tucked the tiny chime beneath his chin like a new singing star.

“Nella likes music,” Mina told Jojo. “She just likes it gentle.” Jojo blinked at the reeds. “So louder is not better?” Pip’s throat puffed, then softened. “I can croak like a moonbeam,” he said, looking rather proud of the impossible idea.

Pip drew himself up for a mighty boom, then remembered Nella’s hidden ears. “Tonight,” he announced, “I will croak softly enough for a firefly to keep its nap.” Jojo surrendered a little gasp of surprise. Mina squeezed Pip’s shoulder. The frog’s grandest promise became a tiny, brave one.

Pip sang a soft, wobbly croak. Nella answered with the shell chime: ting, ting, tingle. Mina swayed, Jojo held his breath, and the reed bells nodded along. When the last note faded, Nella’s smile was wide enough to catch a moonbeam.

The concert ended with Pip’s gentle croak, Nella’s bright response, and the reed bells whispering around them. Mina leaned against Jojo, Jojo grinned at Pip, and the pond held their new little rhythm as carefully as a snail holds a shell.
