## The Pond Concert That Listened — seed 1000

Ada wanted Mira to watch Frog perform, but the invitation was still undelivered. The pond breeze nudged the bank, Ben’s lily-pad stage waited unfinished, and Frog puffed out his cheeks. “This will be a lively concert,” he promised, while one loose leaf sailed past like a tiny audience member.

Ada noticed that Mira wanted to watch. Ada placed the invitation card in Mira’s hands. Ada told Frog that Mira wanted to watch the concert.

Mira received the invitation, and Ada’s delivery was complete.

After delivering the invitation, Ada walked from the cove to the pond.

Ada lent Ben her stage ribbon. “Use it to hold things steady,” she said. Ben carried Ada’s ribbon from the bank to the pond. Ben noticed that the pond was breezy.

Ben used the ribbon to secure the stage against the breeze.

With the ribbon holding fast, Ben prepared the floating stage. It bobbed, but it did not scoot away like a lily-pad hat in a windstorm.

Ben nudged the ready lily-pad stage from the bank onto the still pond.

Frog softened his lively act into a gentle pond melody. Mira watched from the cove as the notes drifted over the water, each one small enough to fit beneath a dragonfly’s wing.

Mira watched Frog’s gentle pond melody from the cove as the breeze combed the pond into silver lines. Ben’s stage stayed secure with Ada’s ribbon, Ada remained at the pond, and the delivered invitation had done its job. The final note floated by like a feather that knew where to land.

## The Pond Concert That Listened — seed 1001

Ada clutched her invitation card in Quiet Cove. She wanted to deliver it to Mira before the pond concert, but Mira wanted quiet, not a noisy frog fanfare. At the pond, the water lay still, while Ben’s floating lily-pad stage waited unfinished and Frog practiced a very lively “RIBBIT!”

Ada noticed Mira’s wish for quiet.

Ada paused. “No booming ribbits for Mira,” she decided. She chose to make room for quiet instead of assuming everyone wanted a noisy concert.

Ada placed the invitation card in Mira’s hands.

Mira received the invitation, and Ada’s delivery was complete.

Ben looked across the pond and noticed that the water was still. Ada told Frog that Mira wanted quiet.

“Still water—excellent!” said Ben. He fitted the lily pads together and prepared the floating stage without needing a ribbon or a tug-of-war with the pond.

Ben nudged the ready lily-pad stage from the bank onto the still pond.

Frog lowered his lively voice to a soft ripple-song. Tiny rings spread from the stage, and Mira heard music gentle enough to leave the cove’s quiet undisturbed.

Mira listened from Quiet Cove while the pond carried Frog’s soft ripple-song in silver circles. Ada stayed nearby, Ben’s stage rested on calm water, and no ribbon had to leave the bank. The invitation had opened a concert that sounded less like a shout and more like a secret tickle on the pond.
