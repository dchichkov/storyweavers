## The Parade That Carried Good News — seed 1000

Ada wanted to deliver a library book to Nell, who hoped for quiet in the square. Bram’s cart was damaged, and its cover was loose; even the breeze seemed ready to make matters worse. Mayor Bell’s promised biggest parade suddenly had a very wobbly beginning.

Ada noticed the breeze tugging ribbons in the square. “That wind is breezy,” she said, watching a scrap of paper scoot past like it had somewhere important to be. Bram checked his cart and found the damage: one wheel wobbled, and the cart gave a sadly lopsided creak. “You are not ready,” he told it. The cart seemed to agree. Ada told Bram about the breeze. Bram looked at the cart’s loose cover. “Then the wind may borrow the cover,” he said, “unless I tie it down first.”

Bram tightened the loose wheel and tested the cart with a careful push. This time it rolled straight. “Ready,” he announced. “Now you can carry something more useful than my lunch.”

Ada noticed Nell watching the noisy preparations. “I hope Nell wants quiet,” Ada whispered. Nell nodded. That was the exact thing she wanted. Ada told Mayor Bell, “Nell wants quiet.” Mayor Bell lowered the parade horn from his lips. “Quiet?” he said. “That is a very small sound—and perhaps a very important one.”

Bram tied the cart’s loose cover firmly. He tugged the knot twice. The cover stayed put, even when the breeze gave it a testing flap.

Bram lent Ada the cart after securing its cover. “Now the wind can fuss all it likes,” he said. “It may flap, but it may not fly away with our cargo.” Ada took Bram’s borrowed cart from the workshop to the library, where the book waited. Its wheels bumped over the floorboards with a polite little clop-clop. With the book tucked safely aboard, Ada rolled Bram’s cart from the library to the town square. She kept both hands on the handle and steered around every pebble.

Ada delivered the book to Nell beneath the tied cover. The breeze rustled above them, but the pages stayed safe. Nell accepted the book with a grateful smile.

Mayor Bell saw that the book now belonged to Nell. He looked at the book, then at his enormous horn, as if the horn had suddenly grown rather silly.

Mayor Bell led a quiet book procession. No drum boomed; no horn blared. He walked proudly while Nell held her book, and the whole parade made only the soft shush of shoes.

The breeze danced around the square, lifting ribbons but not the cart’s tied cover. Under it, Nell held her book safely while Mayor Bell led a proud, quiet procession. The cart’s knots made tiny taps in the wind, like two little drumbeats keeping everyone calm.
