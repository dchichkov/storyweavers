## The Pond Concert Promise — seed 1000

Ada wanted to invite Mira to Frog’s pond concert, and Ben wanted to help. Yet Mira wanted quiet while a breeze skimmed the pond, and Ada’s wide lily pad still waited in the garden. Frog’s acorn drum waited too, although it belonged to Frog and not to Ada.

Ada peeked toward Mira’s porch and noticed Mira sitting with a book, enjoying quiet. “Quiet,” Ada whispered, “is Mira’s wish today.”

Ada picked up the painted invitation and carried it from the garden to the porch. She held it carefully; the blue frog on its front had very important-looking spots.

Ada told Frog, “Mira wants quiet today.” Frog nodded. “Then my drum shall use its teeny-tiniest toes.”

With the invitation waiting on the porch, Ada walked up from the garden. She practiced her polite knock, then decided one knock was plenty.

Frog carried his acorn drum from the garden to the pond. He set it near the water, where its round wooden belly was ready to go bonk—or perhaps merely bonk politely.

Knowing Mira wanted quiet, Frog played a gentle song on the acorn drum. Tap, hush, tap—each note skimmed the pond like a tiny pebble that had learned manners.

Mira smiled and listened to Frog’s gentle pond song. She stayed on the porch, quiet as a sleeping feather, while the soft taps reached her.

Ada gave Mira the painted invitation. “A pond concert,” she said, “with a very small volume knob.” Mira accepted it from Ada with a curious smile.

The invitation was safely delivered: Mira now owned the painted card, and Ada’s delivery was complete. The blue frog on the card seemed pleased with its new address.

Mira smiled and listened as Frog’s gentle notes floated across the still pond. Ada’s invitation rested safely in Mira’s hands, and the acorn drum made no thunder at all. In the evening light, each quiet ripple looked like a little silver ear, listening too.

## The Pond Concert Promise — seed 1002

Ada wanted to invite Mira to Frog’s pond concert, and Ben wanted to help. Yet Mira wanted quiet while a breeze skimmed the pond, and Ada’s wide lily pad still waited in the garden. Frog’s acorn drum waited too, although it belonged to Frog and not to Ada.

Frog noticed a breeze ruffling the pond. “The water is tickling the reeds,” he said. “A lily pad might go sailing without buying a ticket.”

Ada picked up the painted invitation and carried it from the garden to the porch. She held it carefully; the blue frog on its front had very important-looking spots.

Ada peeked toward Mira’s porch and noticed Mira sitting with a book, enjoying quiet. “Quiet,” Ada whispered, “is Mira’s wish today.” Ada pushed her wide lily pad from the garden onto the pond. It floated broad and steady, making a perfect little platform for Frog’s planned rhythm. Ada told Frog, “Mira wants quiet today.” Frog nodded. “Then my drum shall use its teeny-tiniest toes.”

With the invitation waiting on the porch, Ada walked up from the garden. She practiced her polite knock, then decided one knock was plenty.

Ada gave Mira the painted invitation. “A pond concert,” she said, “with a very small volume knob.” Mira accepted it from Ada with a curious smile.

Frog told Ada about the breezy pond. Ada looked at the waving reeds and nodded. “Then we’ll give my lily pad something sturdy to hold.”

The invitation was safely delivered: Mira now owned the painted card, and Ada’s delivery was complete. The blue frog on the card seemed pleased with its new address.

Ada and Ben anchored the lily pad against the breeze. Ben held the cord while Ada pressed the anchor down; the pad stopped drifting and stayed put.

Frog carried his acorn drum from the garden to the pond. He set it near the water, where its round wooden belly was ready to go bonk—or perhaps merely bonk politely.

Knowing Mira wanted quiet, Frog played a gentle song on the acorn drum. Tap, hush, tap—each note skimmed the pond like a tiny pebble that had learned manners.

Mira smiled and listened to Frog’s gentle pond song. She stayed on the porch, quiet as a sleeping feather, while the soft taps reached her.

Mira smiled and listened to Frog’s gentle song while the anchored lily pad held steady in the breezy pond. Ben’s helping hands had made a difference, and Ada’s invitation rested with Mira. Across the water, soft notes trembled like tiny lanterns tied safely to their strings.
