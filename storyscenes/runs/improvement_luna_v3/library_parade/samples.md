## The Library Parade and the Cart with Three Jobs — seed 1000

The parade lane glittered with paper stars, and Mayor Mallow stretched her arms toward the sky. “Biggest parade ever!” she announced. Nia hugged her library list, and Tuck patted the cart’s sturdy handle. Somewhere between a grand float, a book journey, and a cozy reading room waited the cart’s real job.

Nia waved her invitation like a tiny flag. “Mayor Mallow! Tuck! Meet me in the square—there are children waiting for a library surprise.” The mayor came jingling with plans, and Tuck rolled after her, eager to discover which job his wooden cart would choose.

Tuck tapped the cart’s side. “I know something you don’t,” he said. With a careful tug, he revealed hidden folding shelves. “This cart can open into a little room.” Mallow stared. The cart had been keeping a secret as neatly as a bookmark keeps a place.

At the neighborhood stop, Tuck placed the loaded cart’s handle into Nia’s hands. “Take it where the stories are,” he said. Nia nodded, suddenly keeper of wheels, books, and a wonderfully serious bell. The cart gave one proud wooden creak.

Together they leaned into the handles, and the cart rolled toward the parade lane with a friendly wooden rumble. Nia steered past the parade flags toward the neighborhood stop, where small faces were already peeking from doorways.

At the neighborhood stop, the loaded cart waited like a friendly little library on wheels. The bell hung ready beside the books, and a child peeped around the corner, already reaching for a story.

Mallow peered into the cart and found it empty—so empty that a lost button could have held a parade in it. “Excellent!” she cried. “It shall become my enormous float.” Nia blinked at the bare boards, while Tuck’s eyebrows lifted like two curious caterpillars.

Mallow tied her enormous banner to the empty cart. It climbed above her like a cheerful red sail. “Behold!” she cried. “A float so grand it needs its own introduction!” Tuck rang no bell yet, but he did give the cart an approving pat.

The cart stood in the parade lane as a towering float, its enormous banner lifting above the stones like a bright red sail. Mallow beamed at it, while Tuck gave the wooden handle an affectionate pat.

Mallow noticed the library books waiting nearby, their pages puffed up like little wings. She had thought only of cheering crowds, but these books seemed to be waiting for children who had not arrived yet. “Well,” she murmured, “that changes the shape of a parade.”

For the cozy gathering, Nia lifted the books out and made a bright little nest of stories.

Tuck pulled, clicked, and unfolded. Shelves sprang out; a little reading nook appeared. Mallow’s mouth made a perfect O. “My cart is a room!” she whispered. Nia set down the books, and suddenly the parade had somewhere wonderful to stop.

The cart found its grandest job in the square: not merely rolling past the crowd, but gathering the crowd close. Its shelves stood open, its books shone, and every child leaned toward the next page.
