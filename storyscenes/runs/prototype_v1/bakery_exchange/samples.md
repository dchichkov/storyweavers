## The Crumb-and-Label Exchange — seed 1000

At the sunny market stall, the buns looked delicious—but the label did not make everything clear. Ada glanced from proud Pip to hungry Noor and decided that careful checking would be better than guessing.

Ada leaned over the tray and examined the paper label. She frowned thoughtfully: a label could be neat and still be wrong. Ada discovered that it was not accurate.

Ada turned to Noor. “Which filling will be gentle and filling for you?” Noor answered clearly, and Ada listened instead of making a guess.

Ada showed Mara what she had checked and explained Noor's need. “Now we can make a fair choice,” she said. Mara could see why the arrangement needed care.

Ada told Mara what the label seemed to say. Mara listened carefully, though she knew that Ada's first clue might not be the whole truth. Now Mara knew what Ada had observed, too.

Ada placed the buns on the little scale and compared their shapes and weights. “Evidence first,” she announced, as the pointer bobbed and settled. Ada still waited for Mara's permission before rearranging anything.

Mara considered Ada's evidence, then nodded. “I wanted to be sure,” she explained. “Now I agree you may arrange the share.”

Ada placed the plain bun in Noor's basket portion. Noor's shoulders loosened, and the hungry neighbor finally had food that suited them. Noor received a plain bun, while the other buns stayed accounted for.

Pip puffed out his little chest. “That nut bun is the one I saved!” he declared, making sure everyone heard his promise.

Since Pip had claimed the bun for a promise, Mara reserved it for him. Pip relaxed, pleased that his word had been remembered. Pip's promised nut bun remained his, while the sharing problem continued to be solved around it.

Ada and Mara set out the checked label beside the tray. The display was no longer confusing: its words matched the buns. Pip's choice and Noor's portion could now be seen without confusion.

By the end, Noor held the plain bun, Pip's saved nut bun was still his, and the stall's display was clear and accurate. The fresh label and the basket made the change visible: no bun had vanished, and nobody had to rely on a proud guess.
