## The Library Cart Parade — seed 1000

In the little town library, Mayor Bramble wanted a parade that looked enormous. Ada, who noticed loose wheels and lopsided books, wondered whether a grand parade might be measured another way. Toma, the careful cart builder, was ready to inspect every useful detail. Together they would discover what the cart, the route, and the readers actually needed.

For the sloping stretch, the keeper lent Toma a sturdy wheel chock. Toma tested it against the floor. “This will hold the cart while we check each movement,” he explained, and tucked it beside the handle.

Mayor Bramble spread a huge sketch across a library table. “The grandest parade needs the biggest-looking cart!” he declared. Ada leaned close instead of clapping. The drawing made her curious about what the real cart could safely carry.

Ada went to the library doorway and studied the route to the square. She discovered that it was open. “Now we know what the wheels will meet,” she told the others, returning with bright, practical eyes.

Ada knelt beside the cart and looked carefully at how the books sat. They had a poor arrangement, not quite as steady as the mayor's splendid picture suggested. “Books have centers of weight,” she murmured, tapping the floor thoughtfully.

Ada asked the readers rather than guessing what they wanted. Some readers needed an easier way to join. Mayor Bramble listened from behind a stack of atlases. His proud expression softened. “Then the sign should welcome people, not merely impress them,” he said.

Ada and Mayor Bramble changed the sign together. The old boast about being the “biggest” disappeared, replaced by an invitation: “Books for everyone.” Bramble held it up and smiled. “That is a promise I can be proud of,” he said.

The keeper brought Toma a bundle of cushions. “Use these between the covers and the cart boards,” she advised. Toma accepted them with both hands. “Good protection is part of good building,” he said.

Toma crouched and measured the cart wheel. The rim was sound beneath his measuring fingers. “A cart can be handsome and still need careful work,” he said. Ada nodded as the wheel gave a tiny squeak.

Ada and Toma took the books out, placed cushions between them, and rearranged the load. Heavy books went low; lighter ones rested above. The cart now carried 2 books in a good balance, and every cover was protected. “Not biggest,” Ada said. “Ready.”

The keeper handed Ada a coil of rope. “For the tight lane,” she said. Ada ran it through her fingers and answered, “We will guide the cart slowly, not tug it wildly.” The rope became one more careful hand.

Before any parade began, Ada and Toma tested the cart on the level beginning of the route. They moved it a little, stopped, listened, and checked the books. The wheels rolled smoothly. Ada grinned. “Testing tells us what imagining cannot.”

The group guided the balanced cart along the open route. Toma watched the wheels, Ada watched the books, and Bramble watched faces turning toward the invitation. When they reached the square, every book was still safe and ready to be opened. At the town square, the finished arrangement was a square reading parade. The cart stood steady, its protected books displayed where people could reach them. Mayor Bramble touched the new sign and laughed softly. Ada opened the first book, Toma settled the cart, and the very first reader leaned in.
