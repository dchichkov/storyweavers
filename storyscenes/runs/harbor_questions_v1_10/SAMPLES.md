Ari had made room on the handcart for a crate of stone slabs. There was a large crate on the workshop floor, which was almost the same thing. Ari looked from it to Cy. Cy had two perfectly ordinary arms, but at the moment they seemed more promising than Ari's own.

"Could you put that on the cart?" Ari asked. "It will be heavy."
"Will it?" said Cy.
"It is a stone crate."
"That tells me what you call it." Cy stayed beside the crate. "Shall we find out what is in it?"

Ari looked into the crate. There was the wooden bottom, with nothing on it. The stone slabs existed, for the moment, only in Ari's instructions.

"I asked you to carry the stones before I had found any stones," Ari said.
"You did."
"I can manage the box."
"That," Cy said, "is a different request. It seems to be addressed to you."

Ari picked up the empty crate and carried it to the workbench. It was not nearly as impressive a feat as the one Ari had requested. It was, however, a useful empty box.

The empty crate stood on the bench. Ari looked into it once more. There was plenty of room, now that the imaginary stones were out.

Q: What was Ari trying to arrange?
A: Ari wanted the crate loaded onto the handcart. Ari believed it held stone slabs and wanted help carrying it.

Q: What did Ari ask Cy to do?
A: Ari asked Cy to load the crate onto the cart. Cy suggested checking its contents before doing that.

Q: Who checked, and what did the inspection establish?
A: Ari found an empty crate. That contradicted Ari's original assumption.

Q: What did Ari do with the new information?
A: Ari withdrew the request for Cy to load the crate. Ari would carry the empty box instead.

Q: What was actually done after the decision?
A: Ari carried the empty crate to the workbench. Cy did not have to perform the requested loading.

Q: What was different at the end?
A: The crate was empty and now stood on the bench. Ari had changed the request after learning that the expected stones were not inside.

The picnic boat needed cups. Ari found a box in the workshop and began planning where it should go before looking inside it. There was room beside the boat for one more useful thing, and Ari was pleased to have found it.

"Will you put the box on the boat?" Ari asked. "Then the cups are dealt with."
"Are they?" said Cy.
"They will be when you put it there."
Cy looked at the box. "I was thinking of an earlier step."

Ari looked inside the box. There were the paper cups, nested one inside another. The useful little spaces had been packed together into a very small space indeed.

"Cups," Ari said. "As requested."
"As checked," Cy answered.
Ari smiled. "Both, then. Will you take them?" This time the question included a real box of cups.

Cy lifted the box onto the picnic boat. The cups went with it, still nested together. Ari did not have to imagine them being there this time.

The box sat aboard the boat, full of exactly what Ari had said it was. Being right after a question felt quieter than being right before one.

A: Ari wanted cups put onto the picnic boat. Ari believed the workshop box contained them.

A: Ari asked Cy to put the box on the boat. Cy wanted the contents checked first.

Q: Who checked, and what did the inspection establish?
A: Ari found paper cups inside the box. That supported Ari's original assumption.

Q: What did Ari do with the new information?
A: Ari kept the original plan after the inspection supported it. Cy then accepted the request.

Q: What was actually done after the decision?
A: Cy put the box of cups onto the picnic boat. That completed the request.

Ari wanted a parcel taken to the jetty. From the workshop, the footbridge was the short way; the lane past the sheds was the long way. In Ari's mind, the parcel was already halfway across the bridge. Cy, who would have to carry it, was still standing on the floor.

"Will you take it over? The bridge will save you a walk."
"A longer walk," Cy said. "It will still be a walk."
"A much shorter one."
Cy looked toward the crossing. "Let us make sure it goes all the way across."

Cy checked the footbridge. The barrier was open and the crossing was clear. For once, the short route in Ari's head matched the route outside.

Cy told Ari what the inspection had shown. Ari listened to the whole answer before reaching for anything. "I asked you for help," Ari said. "I suppose that includes telling me what you find."

"You can take the bridge," Ari said.
"Now I can," Cy answered.
Ari looked at the clear crossing. Waiting to check had not made the bridge any longer.

Cy picked up the parcel, crossed the footbridge and set it down at the jetty. The route was short, as Ari had hoped. The delivery was real, which mattered more.

At the jetty, the parcel rested where it belonged. The bridge behind Cy had carried a person and a parcel, not merely a good idea.

A: Ari kept the original plan after the inspection supported it. Cy then accepted the request.

A tied parcel of paper flags waited in the workshop. Ari had decided that opening it was a job for scissors. Cy had a pair. This made Cy, for the moment, the most useful person in the harbor.

"May I have the scissors?" Ari asked.
"What are you cutting?"
"Whatever is keeping this parcel shut."
Cy did not pass them over yet. "It might be something we want to keep."

Cy examined the fastening and found the loose end of a bow. It had looked rather permanent from farther away. Close up, it offered a place to start.

Cy told Ari what the inspection had shown. Ari listened to the whole answer before reaching for anything. "I asked you for help," Ari said. "I suppose that includes telling me what you find."

"Keep the scissors," Ari said. "I think my fingers can do this."
"They have been available all morning," Cy said.
Ari took hold of the loose end instead of the blades.

Ari pulled the loose end. The bow came free and the parcel opened. Ari carried it to the bench, with the paper flags inside and the scissors still in Cy's hand.

The flags lay in their opened parcel, and the bow no longer held anything shut. The scissors had done nothing at all. Ari gave them a grateful look anyway.
