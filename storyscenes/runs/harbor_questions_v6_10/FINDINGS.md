## What actually changed

- Create Cy's promise to Bo while both are present. Bo travels to the jetty;
  the key remains in Cy's custody until an actual handoff.
- A confirmed heavy carrying job can conflict with that promise. An embodied
  priority determines refusal versus helping first. Refusal is attributed to
  the enacted promise, not to inability or missing authority.
- After refusal, Ari can put the light cup box aboard alone. The same physical
  capacity limit prevents pretending that the heavy stone crate was loaded.
- Delivery to Bo's own destination and a brief scissors handoff do not create
  false conflicts. Cutting is Ari's work, not a reason for Cy to remain behind.
- Returning the key late records actual late repair without erasing breach.
  Promise fulfillment requires the appropriate physical receipt and a closed
  deadline interval, not the presence of a sentence saying it happened.
- An unknown bridge result remains unknown. A separately declared positive
  belief about the lane permits that route; the bridge still requires positive
  inspection evidence, even when its hidden physical state happens to be open.

These are authored, bounded policies with declared time costs, not a general
planner or a learned duration model. Checking consumes one tick, a carrying
job two more, and each Cy route leg one. A brief handoff adds no job interval.
The scripts use existing physical, epistemic and social operations. No shared
capability laws, older engine behavior or runtime-LLM prohibition changed.
The work advances the physical-carrier north star by making actual custody,
knowledge and an actor's commitment determine continuations and prose.

## Manual QA review

Keep all raw grades, including disagreements. Terra accepts all sixty answers;
Luna rejects four. Visible-text inspection gives these qualifications:

- `14007/c002`: the story starts with Ari believing scissors are needed. Finding
  an untieable bow and opening it by hand contradicts that necessity. The judge's
  denial appears mistaken, not a reason to change the causal answer.
- `14005/c003`: Cy's desire to check is conveyed indirectly by the exchange
  about an earlier step. This is an inference, unlike a literal stated desire;
  retain that qualification instead of treating the judgment as certain error.
- `14009/c002`: the submitted answer says **Cy**, not Ari, accepted the request.
  Cy also explicitly says the task will be done first and performs it. The
  rejection misattributes the submitted answer.
- `14000/c005`: Cy agrees that help is needed and then loads the crate. Saying
  Cy accepted is supported by conduct, although there is no literal acceptance
  sentence. The judge's claim that Ari accepted is not in the answer either.

No scores were manually raised. These observations do not make every other QA
answer correct; local branch review remains necessary.
