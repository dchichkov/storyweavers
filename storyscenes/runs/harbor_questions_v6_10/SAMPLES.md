A space on the handcart was waiting for a crate of stone slabs. There was a large crate on the workshop floor, which was almost the same thing. Ari looked from it to Cy. Cy had two perfectly ordinary arms, but at the moment they seemed more promising than Ari's own.

"Could you put that on the cart?" Ari asked. "It will be heavy."
"Will it?" said Cy.
"It is a stone crate."
"That tells me what you call it." Cy stayed beside the crate. "Shall we find out what is in it?"

Cy looked into the crate. Stone slabs lay inside, with hardly any space between them. It was a load of real stones, not merely a large box with an important name.

Cy told Ari what the inspection had shown. Ari listened to the whole answer before reaching for anything. "I asked you for help," Ari said. "I suppose that includes telling me what you find."

"So I do need help," Ari said.
"Yes," said Cy. "Now we know what with."
Ari moved aside to leave room by the crate. The request sounded smaller now, and more useful.

Cy lifted the loaded crate onto the handcart. Ari watched the stone slabs arrive where they were meant to be, inside a crate rather than inside an argument. "Thank you," Ari said. This was easier than giving instructions.

The cart held the stones. Beside it, Ari had two perfectly ordinary arms again, and someone to ask when they were not enough.
