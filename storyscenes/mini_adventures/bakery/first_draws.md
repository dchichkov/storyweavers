Nell hid a paper crown under the village bakery counter. Tomorrow Mr Vale was retiring to the sea; tonight the village would surprise him with supper. Their plaited loaf waited in its tin. The windowless oven-room bulb had failed; the baker was fetching another from town. "His lantern," Kit suggested. "Ask without telling," Nell said. "Seven o'clock. Not a word."

They crossed the lane to the workshop. "Could we borrow your lantern? Our bulb's gone," Nell asked. Vale lifted it off its hook. "Bring it back before I lock up. I need it for the cellar steps." Nell took the folded handle. Back across the lane, Kit held the bakery door for her and the borrowed light.

They carried the lantern into the oven room. The wheel stuck when Nell tried it. She eased the bent guard aside; it turned. She lit it and set it on the stone shelf, clear of the towels. Kit helped slide the loaf into the oven. "Everybody's bread," he said. Nell closed the door gently. For ten minutes the plaits swelled in the heat.

A knock reached them from the bakery. Nell extinguished the lantern and carried it out to answer. "The van's early. I need my lantern now," Vale said. Behind Nell, the oven ticked. The loaf needed ten more minutes, and she needed light to check it. Kit looked at her, then at the crown hidden beneath the counter.

"There's a loaf for your leaving supper," Nell said. "It was meant to be a surprise." Vale took off his cap. Kit stopped hiding the crown. "Could you stay and help?" Nell asked. Vale set his key beside the till. "The cellar can wait ten minutes. Let's see what your loaf needs." The secret was out.

Vale followed them into the oven room. Nell gave him the lantern; he held it while she relit it. After ten minutes Nell opened the oven and tapped the loaf: hollow. Together they lifted it onto the rack, turned the oven off, and extinguished the light. Once the loaf cooled, Kit carried it out to the supper table.

Vale kept his lantern hooked over his wrist. There would be no shout of surprise when the others arrived; he was already cutting the heel. "Sorry you found out," she said. "I was going to eat standing up." He gave her half. Kit placed the crown beside the bread, leaving flour on the dark lantern handle.

Nell tried the lantern again; its dry wick would not catch. Nell went into the oven room and turned the oven off without opening it. The baker would finish the loaf when he returned with a bulb. Back at the counter she wrote: Breakfast here before you go? "Tell him it's from both of us," Kit said.

They crossed to the workshop with the invitation, then came back to the bakery. The lantern was back with its owner. "Before you leave," Nell had written under Breakfast. Kit set two cups beside the bread rack. The loaf was still unfinished inside; on the counter the crown waited upside down, keeping a place for a morning they had not had yet.
