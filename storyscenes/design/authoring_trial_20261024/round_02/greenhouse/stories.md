"Can I come in?" Mara asked. Ivo lifted his bucket away from the doorway. "Mind the latch." She opened the door, carried the parcel inside and put it on the dry bench before shutting the door. The dead bean stalks stood in their row. He had not thrown them away yet.

The greenhouse had grown close and breathless. Ivo's young tomatoes drooped towards the hot bench. He pulled the vent cord, but the cord parted and the pane stayed shut. Mara watched a leaf curl against the glass. "It has to stay open," Ivo said. "Not just while someone stands here holding it."

The tray sagged in Mara's hands. "Your parcel string could secure the wooden stay," Ivo said. "Moving them will be quicker." She sheltered the parcel and opened the door. They spent half an hour freeing rooted pots for the shaded lower bench. Leaves wilted first; by the last trip, two seedlings had scorched leaves. They were alive, but those leaves would not recover. The parcel was still tied.

"Not another tray today," Ivo said, looking at the damaged seedlings. She asked for his sandwich tin; Ivo opened it. She unwrapped the beans and spread them inside. She set the open tin on the shelf. "Can I come back to help?" "Saturday. While I am here." She nodded, leaving the beans unsown.

They stepped onto the path and Mara closed the door. Ivo kept the key. "Knock on Saturday." She nodded without asking for it. Through the glass stood an open tin of unsown beans. There would be no coming and going on her own yet. She waited until he had finished speaking before turning towards the gate.
