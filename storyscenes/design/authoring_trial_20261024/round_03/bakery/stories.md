Nell hid a paper crown under the village bakery counter. Tomorrow Mr Vale was retiring to the sea; tonight the village would surprise him with supper. Their plaited loaf waited in its tin. The windowless oven-room bulb had failed; the baker was fetching another from town. "His lantern," Kit suggested. "Ask without telling," Nell said. "Seven o'clock. Not a word."

They crossed the lane to the workshop. "Could we borrow your lantern? Our bulb's gone," Nell asked. Vale lifted it off its hook. "Bring it back before I lock up. I need it for the cellar steps. Leave oil for them." Nell took the handle. Back across the lane, Kit held the bakery door for her and the borrowed light.

Nell checked the oil, then carried the lantern into the oven room. The wheel stuck when she tried it. She eased the bent guard aside; it turned. She lit it and set it on the stone shelf, clear of the towels. Kit helped slide the loaf into the oven. "Everybody's bread," he said. Nell closed the door gently. For ten minutes the plaits swelled in the heat.

A knock reached them from the bakery. Nell extinguished the lantern and carried it out to answer. "The furniture van's early. I need my lantern now," Vale said. The loaf needed ten more minutes. Kit looked at Nell, then at the crown beneath the counter. She kept her hand around the borrowed handle.

"It's a loaf for your leaving supper," Nell said. "Could you stay?" Vale took his cap off, then looked across the lane. "Even for this, I can't keep the furniture van waiting." She handed him the lantern. He checked its moving wick and remaining oil. "Enough for the steps. Thank you." He went back to his workshop.

Only Vale's cellar oil remained. Nell went in and switched the oven off. Back at the counter she left the baker a note: Can this loaf be saved? Then she wrote Vale another: Breakfast before you leave tomorrow, if we can manage it? Kit read both. "Don't promise bread yet." She underlined the question mark.

They delivered the invitation across the lane, then returned. Vale already had his working lantern and cellar oil. Breakfast was only a question in his pocket. Kit set two cups on the counter. Nell laid the crown between them. The loaf was still unfinished; the unanswered note for the baker lay beside the empty places.

The windowless oven-room bulb had failed; the baker was fetching another from town. "His lantern," Kit suggested. "Ask without telling," Nell said. "Seven o'clock. Not a word."

They crossed the lane to the workshop. "Could we borrow your lantern? Our bulb's gone," Nell asked. Vale lifted it off its hook. "Bring it back before I lock up. I need it for the cellar steps. Leave oil for them." Nell took the handle. Back across the lane, Kit held the bakery door for her and the borrowed light.

Nell checked the oil, then carried the lantern into the oven room. The wheel stuck when she tried it. She eased the bent guard aside; it turned. She lit it and set it on the stone shelf, clear of the towels. Kit helped slide the loaf into the oven. "Everybody's bread," he said. Nell closed the door gently. For ten minutes the plaits swelled in the heat.

A knock reached them from the bakery. Nell extinguished the lantern and carried it out to answer. "Nearly locking-up time. May I have my lantern?" Vale asked. The loaf needed ten more minutes. Kit looked at Nell, then at the crown beneath the counter. She kept her hand around the borrowed handle.

"Could we keep it a little longer?" Nell asked. "Then I'll bring it straight over." Vale checked his watch. "Ten. I can pack the counter first." He went back across the lane, leaving the lantern in her hand. Kit let out his breath. Nell held up both hands, counting the minutes on her fingers.

They carried the lantern back into the oven room and relit it. Kit watched the clock. After ten minutes Nell opened the oven and tapped the loaf: hollow. Together they lifted it onto the rack, turned the oven off, and extinguished the light. Using towels around the hot tin, Kit carried the loaf to the table.

They returned the lantern across the lane. Vale checked its wick and cellar oil, then followed their invitation back. At the bakery Nell stepped aside: the loaf stood on the table. "For me?" Kit offered the crown. Vale rested the lantern at his place while Kit broke the heel. "Keep me the crust," he said.

The windowless oven-room bulb had failed; the baker was fetching another from town. "His lantern," Kit suggested. "Ask without telling," Nell said. "Seven o'clock. Not a word."

They crossed the lane to the workshop. "Could we borrow your lantern? Our bulb's gone," Nell asked. Vale lifted it off its hook. "Bring it back before I lock up. I need it for the cellar steps. Leave oil for them." Nell took the handle. Back across the lane, Kit held the bakery door for her and the borrowed light.

Kit pointed to the lantern's brass wheel. "Is that the light?" Nell tried it while the glass was cold. It turned smoothly, lifting a little black edge. "That works," Nell said. Kit put his hands in his pockets. "I thought you just asked it nicely." "You can try that with the oven."

Nell checked the oil, then carried the lantern into the oven room. She turned the wheel; the wick rose. She lit it and set it on the stone shelf, clear of the towels. Kit helped slide the loaf into the oven. "Everybody's bread," he said. Nell closed the door gently. For ten minutes the plaits swelled in the heat.

A knock reached them from the bakery. Nell extinguished the lantern and carried it out to answer. "Nearly locking-up time. May I have my lantern?" Vale asked. The loaf needed ten more minutes. Kit looked at Nell, then at the crown beneath the counter. She kept her hand around the borrowed handle.

"Could we keep it a little longer?" Nell asked. "Then I'll bring it straight over." Vale checked his watch. "Ten. I can pack the counter first." He went back across the lane, leaving the lantern in her hand. Kit let out his breath. Nell held up both hands, counting the minutes on her fingers.

They carried the lantern back into the oven room and relit it. Kit watched the clock. After ten minutes Nell opened the oven and tapped the loaf: hollow. Together they lifted it onto the rack, turned the oven off, and extinguished the light. Using towels around the hot tin, Kit carried the loaf to the table.

They returned the lantern across the lane. Vale checked its wick and cellar oil, then followed their invitation back. At the bakery Nell stepped aside: the loaf stood on the table. "For me?" Kit offered the crown. Vale rested the lantern at his place while Kit broke the heel. "Keep me the crust," he said.
