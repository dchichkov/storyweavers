# The Smallest Sea: Generated Stories

## Seed 0

Tom stood by the back door in boots and a paper hat. "I'm captain. You're the crew."
"Explorer," Lily said. "I choose things too."
Mom brought a map. "Treasure under the apple tree. No trampling my flowers." Lily took it.
"Even for treasure?" Tom asked.
"Especially for treasure."

Outside, they passed the summerhouse. Tom opened its door. "A cave. We should explore it."
"The treasure's at the tree," Lily said.
"There might be two treasures."
They went inside. Open shutters striped the floor with sunlight. Tom switched on the light. A coat hung above an empty deckchair.

Tom switched off the light. "A proper cave!"
Lily looked at the sunny floor. "Your cave has windows."
Tom held both hands up to make a shadow. Lily made a bigger one beside it.
"Mine wins."
Tom clicked the light back on. "Next time we need a cave without windows."

"No treasure," Lily said. "Let's try the tree." Tom turned out the light. They stepped into the garden and pulled the door shut. The latch clicked. Tom rattled the handle once to check. "Cave secured."
Lily tucked the map under an arm. "Off we go."

A puddle covered the path to the apple tree. "The sea," Tom said.
Lily helped lay a board across it, both ends resting on dry ground. They pressed it with their boots. It stayed firm.
"One at a time."
They crossed slowly. Neither boot touched the brown water.

Under the apple tree stood an upside-down flowerpot. Tom tugged at it. "Help?" Lily took the other side. Together they set it aside.
Lily picked up the biscuit tin underneath. Tom eased the lid open.
Inside were two ginger biscuits, one broken.
"Gold?"
"Better. You can eat this."

Tom broke the whole biscuit in half. Lily divided the broken pieces into two piles.
"Which do you want?"
"That one has a bigger crumb."
Tom moved the crumb to the other pile. They looked again, then laughed. Both took a share. Soon only ginger crumbs remained on their fingers.

They crossed the board again, carrying the empty tin. At home, boots came off and the tin went on the table. On the sofa, Lily drew their bridge on the map.
"Biscuit Bridge," the captain decided.
"We both built it."
Tom added two tiny figures beside it, standing shoulder to shoulder.

## Seed 1

Tom stood by the back door in boots and a paper hat. "I'm captain. You're the crew."
"Explorer," Lily said. "I choose things too."
Mom brought a map. "Treasure under the apple tree. No trampling my flowers." Lily took it.
"Even for treasure?" Tom asked.
"Especially for treasure."

Mom glanced at Tom's new red coat. "In that?"
"I've got boots."
Lily pointed above them. "Mud goes higher."
Mom offered an old jacket, but the brass buttons were too splendid to give up.
"I'll be careful," Tom said, fastening the last one.

Lily found a damp paper flag behind the boots. "From our last ship."
"That one sank," Tom said.
"You sat on it."
"It wasn't built for captains."
Lily smoothed the flag. A biscuit crumb still clung to the tape. The flag went on the windowsill to dry.

Lily spread the map on the bench. "Left," Tom announced.
"That's the fence." Lily turned the paper until its little house faced their house.
"Oh." Tom studied it again. "You'd better keep that."
Lily folded it into a pocket, with the red cross safely inside.

Outside, a puddle covered the path. Tom laid down a board; Lily tested it with a boot. Its far end slipped underwater, splashing the captain's red sleeve.
They pulled it out. Lily pointed around the flower bed. "Land all the way."
Tom stepped aside. "Show me." Together they followed the longer path to the apple tree.

Under the apple tree stood an upside-down flowerpot. Tom tugged at it. "Help?" Lily took the other side. Together they set it aside.
Lily picked up the biscuit tin underneath. Tom tried the lid, then dried slippery fingers on the coat. This time the lid came away easily.
Inside were two ginger biscuits, one broken.
"Gold?"
"Better. You can eat this."

"Captains get the whole one," Tom said. Lily stopped reaching.
Tom divided the broken pieces into two piles, then broke the whole biscuit and added half to each.
"Explorers get half."
"Half of which?"
"Both."
Lily checked both piles. They ate beneath the dripping leaves.

They took the long path home, carrying the empty tin. At home, boots came off and the tin went on the table. Tom rubbed the muddy red sleeve. "Should have changed." Lily named the long way Biscuit Passage on the map. Tom offered the paper hat.
"Your turn to be captain."
Lily put it on and made room on the sofa. "Let's choose where to go."
