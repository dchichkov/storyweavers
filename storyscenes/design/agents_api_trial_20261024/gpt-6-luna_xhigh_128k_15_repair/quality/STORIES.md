## s000029: seed 5029, 7 scenes

Jo had promised Mina the toy cart for her seedling run, and its front wheel answered every push with a dry rattle. At Len's courtyard repair bench, Jo asked for the spanner. “Borrow it,” Len said, “but don't make a nut tighter just because it makes noise.” Mina held the cart steady. Jo heard the warning, and the rattle, but trusted the obvious answer.

The sloping bench let the cart creep, so Jo wedged its frame. The borrowed spanner slipped; Len exchanged it for his adjustable one. “Loose,” Jo said. One careful turn pinched the hub against the fork. The wheel stopped turning, but the rattle still clicked from side to side. Mina raised an eyebrow. Jo's fingers stayed on the handle.

The wheel shifted sideways in Mina's hands, although its nut was already pinched tight. Jo crouched and saw daylight where a thin spacer should sit between hub and fork. “So it wasn't loose,” Mina said. “I made it worse,” Jo answered. The missing washer explained both the rattle and the wheel that would no longer turn.

Jo brushed the grit from both palms. “Something's wrong with the cart. Give me a minute.” Mina saw the pinched wheel but didn't argue. “A minute, then we decide together.” Jo wanted to sound certain; instead the silence made the first guess feel heavier. The missing spacer lay plainly between them.

They cleared the light load from the wheel before working. A spare washer waited in Len's parts tin. Jo placed it between hub and fork, then eased the nut until the wheel had room to turn without wobbling. Mina spun it once. The rattle was gone. “That's the sound I wanted,” she said, not the silence of a stuck wheel.

Mina set the two pots in the bed and walked beside Jo. Mina watched closely while Jo pushed alone. They crossed the courtyard to the exchange table. The wheel turned cleanly over the paving; no sideways knock came back. Mina unloaded the seedlings. “Now I can set them out before the neighbors arrive.” Jo finally let go of the handle.

Jo parked the empty cart by the bench. “I should've told you I made it worse,” Jo said. Mina nodded, still watching the wheel. “You did tell me. Next time, sooner.” Len used the returned spanner to snug his loose vise screw. Mina wheeled the seedlings into place, and Jo walked beside her.
