At five o’clock, Mina found the moon atlas she wanted on the library’s display table. She tucked it under her arm. If she borrowed it before closing, she could sketch the craters tonight and show her class tomorrow.

Then Jun hurried over, his cheeks pink from running. He was six, and Mina was nine, which meant he often asked her to reach high shelves and read the trickier words.

“My whale book’s gone,” he said. “The one I picked for Grandma to read tonight. I had it by the window, and now it’s not there.”

Jun’s grandmother was waiting at the front desk. The library closed at six.

“I’ll help you find it,” Mina said. “I promise—before closing.”

The book was called The Whale Who Sang to the Moon. Jun had been carrying it around the children’s room, showing everyone the picture of a blue whale swimming beneath a sky full of stars.

Mina hurried to Ms. Alvarez, the librarian. “Could we borrow the little book trolley to search?”

“You may borrow it,” Ms. Alvarez said, handing her the handle. “Put any stray books you find on it. And keep all four wheels on the floor.”

“I know,” Mina said. She set her moon atlas carefully on the front desk, where she could collect it later, then pushed the empty trolley into the children’s room.

They searched the window nook first. Jun checked behind the cushions while Mina looked beneath the bench. They found a mitten, two board books, and a crayon shaped like a dinosaur. All went onto the trolley.

“Not my whale,” Jun said.

They checked the puppet theater, the rug, and the shelves beside the fish tank. Mina ran her finger along the picture-book spines. Jun crouched to inspect the lowest shelf, whispering the title to himself as if the book might answer.

At five-twenty, the trolley was already carrying a crooked tower of books. Mina stopped it whenever a wheel squeaked and steadied the stack with one hand. They looked through every book they’d collected, but none had a blue whale on the cover.

Jun’s mouth turned down. “Grandma says we have to go when the library closes.”

“We still have time,” Mina said.

They searched the reading tables. Near the astronomy shelf, Jun spotted a silver paper star on the floor. He picked it up and rubbed a crease flat with his thumb.

“My bookmark,” he said. “It was inside the whale book.”

Mina looked at the high shelves above the astronomy books. A silver point glimmered between two thick volumes.

“Is that it?” Jun asked.

“I think so. But we can’t climb.”

Mina glanced at the moon atlas on the desk. She had imagined opening it at home, then drawing the moon in the round corner of her notebook. The clock above the desk said 5:48.

She could ask Ms. Alvarez to put the atlas aside while they searched. But the librarian was checking out a family at the desk, and the silver point might be the bookmark—or might be something else.

Mina took the moon atlas from the desk and carried it to Ms. Alvarez.

“Could you put this back on the shelf?” she asked. “I need to keep looking with Jun.”

Ms. Alvarez studied her face, then nodded. “All right. But I’ll get the ladder.”

While they waited, Jun gripped the trolley handle. “I thought I put the book back,” he said. “I couldn’t reach the picture-book shelf, so I put it on the astronomy shelf. The whale had stars around it.”

Mina looked up at the silver point. “Then it’s probably there.”

Ms. Alvarez returned with the library ladder and climbed carefully. From below, Mina and Jun watched the star bookmark tremble as the librarian eased the thick books apart. A blue cover slid forward.

“The Whale Who Sang to the Moon,” Ms. Alvarez read.

Jun made a sound like a tiny cheer. Ms. Alvarez brought the book down and handed it to him. His bookmark was still tucked inside, just where he had left it.

At five fifty-eight, Ms. Alvarez checked the book out to Jun. Mina pushed the trolley back to its place, its wheels clicking softly over the tiles. Her moon atlas was back on the display shelf.

Outside, Jun’s grandmother opened the whale book beneath the streetlamp. Jun leaned against her side, and Mina leaned in too. On the first page, the painted whale sailed through a sky of silver stars, while Jun’s paper bookmark rested against its tail.
