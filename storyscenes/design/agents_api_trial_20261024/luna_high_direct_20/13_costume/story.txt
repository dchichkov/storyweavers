At 3:12, the costume cupboard smelled of dust, wool, and the oranges someone had eaten during last year’s play. Nia squeezed between a box of hats and a rack of coats. In the narrow mirror on the door, she saw her blue sleeves and the ribbons tied at her wrists.

“I need something that moves,” she told her reflection.

Her role was the Wind in The Sun Comes Home. She wanted the audience to notice her first swirl, the one she had practiced beside her bed until her mother asked her to stop knocking over the lamp.

On the top shelf lay a red cape with gold lining. Nia reached for it, then paused. A paper tag read SCHOOL—RETURN AFTER PLAY.

“Ms. Vale?” she called.

The teacher was outside the cupboard, sorting scripts. “You can borrow it, Nia. Don’t cut it or pin it. And bring it back after the show.”

Nia slipped the cape over her shoulders. It brushed the floor. In the mirror, the gold flashed when she turned.

Behind her, Rafi stood holding a gray robe. Its sleeves covered his hands. A silver crescent was stitched to the chest.

“This says MOON,” he said.

Nia looked at the cast list taped to the wall. “You’re the Sun.”

“I know. Ms. Vale promised me the Sun.” Rafi pinched the crescent between two fingers. “But the yellow tunic is missing, so she gave me this. I’m supposed to walk on and make everyone think, ‘Oh, look, the Moon.’”

He tried to smile, but the robe dragged across the floor.

Nia knew how he felt. Rafi had practiced his bright, booming Sun voice for weeks. He wanted the part to look like his, too.

“Try this,” she said, and lifted the cape from her shoulders.

Rafi shook his head. “That’s yours. Your Wind part needs it.”

“I have these.” Nia raised her ribboned wrists. The ribbons fluttered, but she pictured her big red sweep vanishing. She had imagined the audience following the cape around the stage.

Ms. Vale appeared in the doorway with a bundle of scripts. “Two minutes to places,” she said. Her eyes went from Rafi’s robe to Nia’s cape. “The Sun disk is in the prop box. Use what you’ve got, and please don’t make me sew anything right now.”

She hurried away.

Nia looked at the cape’s gold lining. Then she handed it to Rafi.

“Wear it over the robe. The gold might help.”

Rafi took it carefully. “Then what will you wear?”

“My wind ribbons.”

He put the cape on. It swallowed his shoulders and puddled around his shoes. He took one step, and the hem caught under his heel.

“Not like that,” Nia said. “Gather the front in your hand. Like you’re holding a sunbeam.”

Rafi tried again. This time the cape lifted clear of the floor, though a strip of silver robe still showed underneath.

Nia fetched the cardboard Sun disk from the prop box. Its yellow paint was cracked along one edge. Rafi held it in his free hand. In the mirror, the gold cape glowed around the gray robe, and the disk shone above it.

“Now you look like the Sun wearing a cloudy morning,” Nia said.

Rafi studied the reflection. “That sounds like the beginning of the play.”

The stage manager called, “Places!”

Nia’s stomach gave a small jump. “Ready?”

Rafi tightened his grip on the cape. “Ready.”

From the wings, Nia listened to the first lines. When Rafi’s cue came, he stepped into the light. The cape brushed the floor, but he gathered it up just in time. He raised the cardboard disk and spoke in his big, bright voice.

“I have come to warm the rooftops!”

A few people in the audience leaned forward. Rafi stood taller. The gold lining shone against the gray robe.

Then Nia’s cue arrived. She rushed on, arms sweeping wide. Without the cape, her ribbons flickered like blue sparks. She circled Rafi, just as they had practiced. He opened the cape with one arm, and its red edges billowed behind him. Nia’s ribbons streamed around the gold.

For a moment they looked like one bright, turning thing: sunlight caught in the wind.

When the curtain fell, Rafi was still holding the Sun disk. Nia was breathing hard, and one of her blue ribbons had come loose. She tucked it back into her wrist knot.

They returned the cape to the cupboard together. Rafi hung it on its hook, and Nia straightened the tag so it faced out. In the mirror, the cape rested between his silver crescent and her blue ribbon, its gold lining catching the last stripe of light from the open door.
