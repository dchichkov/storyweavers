The pond in Briar Park was shallow, but its water was the color of strong tea, and the muddy bottom disappeared beneath the green duckweed. Theo’s wooden boat, the June Bug, had a yellow sail patched with a square of blue cloth. His grandfather had helped him sand the hull, and Theo wanted to test the new patch before his grandfather visited on Sunday.

“It’ll sail all the way to the fountain,” he said.

Mina watched the breeze wrinkle the pond. “Maybe start with the near side.”

Theo set the boat on the water. For a few seconds it sailed beautifully. Then a gust spun it around and sent it skimming toward the reeds.

Theo’s smile vanished. “It’s going to get stuck!”

Mina stayed on the gravel path. “I’ll get Mr. Alvarez.”

The park gardener was raking leaves nearby. Mina asked if she could borrow his long-handled net. He looked from her to Theo, then to the boat drifting farther out.

“Of course,” he said. “But keep both feet on the path. The bank is slippery, even where the pond is shallow.”

“I will,” Mina promised.

Mr. Alvarez handed her the net. Its wooden handle was almost as tall as she was, and the hoop at the end was wide enough to catch a whole heap of leaves. Theo walked beside her as she carried it to a level stretch of gravel.

“Tell me where the boat is,” Mina said.

“Just left of that clump of reeds.”

She lowered the hoop until it skimmed the surface and pushed it toward the boat. The wind nudged the June Bug away. Mina reached farther, keeping her shoes planted on the dry path.

The net hoop slid beneath the boat—and snagged with a sharp jerk.

Mina’s arms jolted. The handle bowed over the water. Somewhere below the surface, the hoop had caught on a submerged willow branch.

“Pull!” Theo cried. “It’s getting away!”

He took a quick step toward the edge. Mina caught his sleeve.

“Stay on the path,” she said. “I mean it.”

Theo stopped. The boat bobbed farther from them, its yellow sail flickering between the reeds.

Mina tightened her grip on the handle. If she yanked hard, she might free the hoop. She might also break Mr. Alvarez’s net—or make the slippery edge crumble under her shoes.

“I’m not pulling,” she said.

“But the boat—”

“I know.” She eased the handle down until it rested against the gravel. “I’m going to free the net first. Then we’ll follow the boat from the path.”

The wind pushed the June Bug past the reeds and toward the pond’s stone outlet. Theo watched it go, his fingers curled around the blue cord he’d brought to use as a tether. He had thought the pond was calm enough without it.

Mina studied the net handle. The hoop had caught while she was pushing it forward. She took a step sideways along the path, then gently pushed the handle away from shore. The hoop shifted underwater. She turned the handle a little, then drew it back.

The hoop slid free.

Mina let out a breath she hadn’t noticed she was holding. She lifted the net clear of the water and checked it. A willow twig lay in its mesh. She plucked it out and set it on the gravel.

“Now,” she said, “we walk.”

They followed the curved path around the pond. Theo kept his eyes on the boat, calling out whenever a gust spun it or a reed hid it. The path brought them to the stone outlet, where the water was calm. The June Bug had drifted into the sheltered corner and bumped softly against the stones.

“There,” Theo whispered. “Close to the edge.”

“Close isn’t the same as reachable,” Mina said.

She stood well back on the path and lowered the net. This time, she moved slowly. Theo watched from beside her.

“A little left,” he said. “Now down.”

The hoop slipped around the boat without touching its sail. Mina drew it toward shore, one careful inch at a time. When the net reached the stones, Theo picked the boat out of the mesh, holding it by the hull.

The yellow sail was still upright. Only a few drops clung to the blue patch.

Mr. Alvarez was waiting where they had left him. Mina returned his net with the willow twig tucked through the handle.

“Thank you,” she said.

“Thank you for bringing it back in one piece,” he replied.

Theo tied the blue cord around the boat’s stern, this time with a firm double knot. Then he set the June Bug afloat again. Mina held the other end of the cord while Theo guided the boat away from the stones.

The breeze caught the yellow sail. The boat made a bright little circle on the tea-colored water, with the blue cord drawn gently between Theo’s hands and Mina’s.
