## s000029: seed 5029, 7 scenes

Nia found Tamsin winding a string reel beside the village kite workshop. "May I borrow it for the afternoon? Pavel just moved next door, and I want us to fly together." Tamsin glanced at the frayed edge. "Bring it back with the line wound neatly." Nia promised, and carried the reel as carefully as a cup of tea.

At the workbench, Nia crossed two willow sticks and stretched blue paper over them. The willow bent without splitting. She tied each corner twice, then gave the frame a cautious shake. It looked less like a kite than a promise with a tail. Outside, a strip of cloud slid over the rooftops.

Nia found Pavel watching from his doorstep. "We have a kite. Would you help me with its tail?" He studied the bright paper. "I can do that." She waited while he fetched his jacket; no one hurried him. At the field, Pavel stood close enough to touch the kite, and Nia let him choose their first job.

A spool of red ribbon lay in the workshop basket. Nia offered it to Pavel. He chose a short piece and tied it to the paper tail, testing the knot with one careful tug. "It needs something bright," he said. The ribbon flicked in the breeze, making the kite easier to see against the pale afternoon sky.

Nia kept the reel against her coat. "I will mind the line. Could you guide the tail until it catches air?" Pavel considered this, then took the kite by its lower edge. "I can do that." It was not the same job as holding the spool, but it was his choice, and Nia waited for his nod before stepping back.

Nia ran three quick steps and lifted the kite. Pavel watched and called, "Now!" The line drew taut, then streamed through the reel as the paper climbed above the roofs. Neither child pulled too hard. The kite steadied in the wind, blue against the clouds, while Pavel and Nia grinned at each other.

They carried the kite home together, Pavel still describing the moment its tail caught the breeze. Nia wound the remaining string neatly and returned the reel to Tamsin, ready for another flight. Pavel had guided the kite into the air, and Nia had listened when he said when to run. At the gate, he asked whether they could fly again sometime.

Nia waited at the field gate instead of calling across the lane. "Pavel, would you like to come? You can help me choose when to let the line out." He looked at his shoes. "I could try." Nia held the reel low, not like a prize. Pavel came beside her, and the kite rested between them.

Before trying the full flight, Nia paid out a few arm-lengths of string. Pavel held the tail while she eased the reel, then they traded places for one small pull. The line ran more smoothly when neither child jerked it. Pavel smiled at the kite. "I know what it wants now." Nia nodded and let him keep the reel.

A spool of red ribbon lay in the workshop basket. Nia offered it to Pavel. He chose a short piece and tied it to the paper tail, testing the knot with one careful tug. "It needs something bright," he said. The ribbon flicked in the breeze, making the kite easier to see against the pale afternoon sky.

Nia held out the reel. "Would you like to hold it? You can tell me when the kite needs more line." Pavel hesitated, then curled both hands around the wooden sides. "Not too fast," he said. "I know." Nia moved to the kite, glad that the important job was his, not something she had to explain away.

Nia ran three quick steps and lifted the kite. Pavel called, "Now!" The line drew taut, then streamed through the reel as the paper climbed above the roofs. Neither child pulled too hard. The kite steadied in the wind, blue against the clouds, while Pavel and Nia grinned at each other.

On the walk back, Pavel carried the kite while Nia held the reel. At the workshop, they wound the remaining string in even loops and returned it to Tamsin. "Still good for another kite," she said. Pavel looked up at Nia. He had held the line in the sky, not merely watched it. She had wanted a flight together, and they had one.
