## s000029: seed 5029, 7 scenes

Mara wanted the new girl to feel at home, and the little drum on Ms Vale’s shelf looked like a beginning. “May I borrow it for practice?” Mara asked. Ms Vale passed it over carefully. “Bring it back after, please.” {child} promised, feeling its wooden rim warm in their hands.

June paused just inside the community music room, one hand still on the door. Mara introduced themself without crossing the floor. “You can sit wherever you like,” they said. June nodded, watching the practisers at the far end. Their fingers worried a sleeve, but they came in.

Mara turned one of the waiting chairs slightly sideways and left an open space beside it. “Nobody has to perform from this seat,” they said. June chose the chair nearest the door: close enough to hear, and far enough to leave.

“I’m trying a call-and-answer,” Mara said. “You could tap once, keep time on your knee, listen, or pass. All of those count as being here.” They left the drum between them, not in the newcomer’s lap. June looked at the open space around it and took a breath.

“I think I want to listen,” June said. Mara nodded without bargaining for a different answer. “Listening counts. You can change your mind, or not.” June let out a breath and settled into the chair. The drum stayed with Mara, not placed in their hands.

Mara played a slow pattern for the person who had chosen to listen. June followed its pauses, then said it sounded like rain tapping a window. Mara repeated the rhythm, softer this time. No second drum joined in, but the answer changed how the first one sounded.

June had chosen to listen, and Mara let that answer stand. They carried the borrowed drum back to Ms Vale, who smiled at the pattern they described. June stayed while the chairs were stacked. No duet had happened; still, the room held a shared piece of music.
