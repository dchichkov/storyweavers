## s000029: seed 5029, 7 scenes

Nia wanted June, the new girl on Willow Lane, to fly a kite with her—not watch from behind the gate. At Tomas’s village workshop she pointed to the blue string reel. “May I borrow it for the afternoon?” Tomas turned its wooden handle. “Bring it back wound tight; it was my father’s.” Nia promised, and carried it out carefully.

June opened the gate. “I’ve never flown a kite,” she said. Nia admitted that her first one had landed in a cabbage patch. June laughed, then glanced at the children gathering on the green. “I don’t like everyone watching.” “Then we’ll have our own bit of sky,” Nia said. June stepped out beside her.

At the bench, June found a split spar. Nia used three careful turns from the borrowed reel to lash it; the knot held. They tied on a balanced tail, though the repair spent some of their line. “A mend, not a miracle,” June said, carrying the kite out with Nia.

They carried the kite and reel along the bakery wall to the open common. June stayed beside Nia, not behind her, and held the kite away from the damp hedge. The green was busy at its far end, so they chose the quiet patch near the hawthorn. “This is our bit of sky,” Nia said. June nodded.

The kite hopped once and nosed into the grass. The borrowed line kept it from skittering away. June picked up the tail, careful not to touch the weak spar. “It tried,” Nia said. A gust rattled the hedge; neither child blamed the other for the drop.

They checked the clear line together, watching the gust rather than fighting it. Nia offered June the handle. June’s fingers shook, but she took it and called each lull; Nia steadied the tail. They waited for June’s count before deciding how high to try.

The kite had only hopped across the grass. June asked them not to pull harder. Nia listened. They folded the kite carefully; the borrowed reel had kept it from escaping and brought the loose line home. Tomas took back his wound spool. The girls signed the paper and hung it in the workshop window, where the afternoon light found it.

They brought it down before the gusts returned, rewound the loose line, and carried it back to Tomas. He took his borrowed reel with a nod. They signed beside June’s blue star and set the kite on the workshop rack. The blue paper turned in the afternoon light.
