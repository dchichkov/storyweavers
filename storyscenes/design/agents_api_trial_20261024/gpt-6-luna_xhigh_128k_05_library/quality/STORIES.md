## s000029: seed 5029, 7 scenes

Lio wanted to collect his reserved comic, but Nia was close to tears. The Moon in My Pocket had gone missing, and the library closed at six. “I’ll find it before closing,” he promised. Ms Vale lent him the book trolley. They used it to clear returned books from the aisle, then turned toward the shelves together.

The catalogue still listed the animal-story shelf, but its date was old. “It may be wrong,” Nia said. Lio wrote down the low shelf anyway; an old location was a lead, not a promise. If the book was somewhere else, they would need another clue before the closing bell made the search impossible.

At the desk, no librarian was free to check the reshelving notes. Lio left the title where staff could see it, but no new location appeared. Nia looked at the list instead of blaming him. The trolley was still useful, though a high shelf would stay out of reach unless a staff member and a safe ladder were available.

Lio checked the shelf one book at a time. The catalogue’s low-shelf lead brought them to the animal stories. No silver moon waited there. They returned each book to its place. The wrong location cost a minute, but neither child pretended that an empty shelf was a discovery.

Nia took the search list from Lio. “I can remember things, not just wait for you,” she said. He started to apologize, then stopped and handed her the pencil instead. She chose the next place to check. The list was still only paper, but sharing it changed who was making the decisions.

Nia lifted the window-seat cushion and spotted the silver moon underneath. The book had been tucked into the gap, its cover turned toward the wall. Lio moved it to the trolley. “I knew I read here,” Nia said. She had remembered a place that his list could never have guessed.

Lio handed The Moon in My Pocket to Nia. Nia held the book close. They returned the useful trolley to its bay, with the other books still neatly sorted. His reserved comic had gone back onto the shelf, and he felt disappointed. Nia asked him to hear her favorite page. The promise was kept; the cost of it was real, and so was the friendship.
