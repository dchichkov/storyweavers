## s000029: seed 5029, 7 scenes

At the Red Awning, Nella wanted Mr. Rafi to trust her with a morning at the scale. Mrs. Vale arrived with an empty basket and an order for 1.6 kg of apples for the clinic’s tart. Rafi lent Nella his dial scale. “Bring it back clean—and weighing true,” he said.

The dial trembled, so Nella tapped its rim until the needle steadied. The crate ran low before the needle reached 1.6 kg; it stopped at 1.5 kg. Nella placed the smaller basket beside the slip. She liked how a number made a hard job look simple; the answer on the dial seemed certain.

After another careful tap, Nella set Mr. Rafi’s iron half-kilo weight on the pan. It should read 0.5 kg, but the needle climbed to 0.6 kg. She lifted it and tried again. The same excess returned: the scale added 0.1 kg. The full-looking basket suddenly looked different.

When Mrs. Vale came back, Nella held up the iron weight. “I found the mistake. The scale adds 0.1 kg; your basket is short by 0.2 kg. I should have checked before filling it.” Mrs. Vale studied the dial. “Thank you for telling me. Let’s decide what can honestly go in the tart.”

There were not enough apples left to top up the basket. Mrs. Vale tasted the pears Nella offered, then chose the blend for her tart. They weighed the remaining apples and pears to reach the order’s full amount. “You told me before I had to ask,” she said, and paid.

Nella steadied the loose dial and turned its small zeroing screw. The needle rested at zero; Mr. Rafi’s half-kilo weight landed exactly on its line. Nella wiped apple juice from the pan and returned the scale to its owner. Rafi used it at once to weigh oranges for the next customer. “Useful again,” he said, and Nella could finally breathe.

The clinic tart would have apples and pears instead of apples alone. Mrs. Vale thanked her for speaking up before taking the basket. Mr. Rafi’s scale stood true on its hook, and Nella understood that an honest correction could change an order without making it worthless.
