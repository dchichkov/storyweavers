# The Honest Mark

Nella wanted Mr. Rafi to trust her with the Saturday counter. She was thirteen, quick at tying paper bags, and good at telling a sweet apple from one that would turn soft by noon. But Rafi still asked her to fetch, sweep, and carry crates while he served customers. “I know how to sell,” she told him. “You know how to hurry,” he replied, smiling. “The scale is less forgiving than a customer.”

That morning Mrs. Vale arrived with her market basket and a folded note. She needed a kilo and a half of apples for a tart she was taking to the clinic’s afternoon table. Nella had promised to finish the order before the lunch crowd. Mr. Rafi was unloading oranges at the far end of the stall, so she asked if she could borrow his dial scale. He lifted it by its handle and passed it over. “Bring it back clean, and weighing true. I need it all morning.” Nella nodded as if that were the easiest part.

While Mrs. Vale went to choose cinnamon, a boy named Sam came to the apple crate. He picked up a bruised one and asked whether it was still good. “My mum wants apples for stew. She doesn’t care if it’s pretty.” Nella gave it to him. Sam grinned and ran toward the shoe shop where his family lived. Rafi glanced over but said nothing. Nella liked that he had seen her choose what was useful, not just what looked perfect.

She lined Mrs. Vale’s basket with brown paper and piled in crisp red apples. The needle shivered, then settled at the requested mark. Nella turned the basket so the brightest skins showed. “That’s a fine lot,” Mrs. Vale said. Nella felt a small lift of pride. She had finished quickly, and Rafi had not needed to check behind her. Then she noticed that the pile looked a little lower than she expected. The scale’s number still held steady.

Rafi had once told her that an honest scale began with a known weight. Nella took his iron half-kilo block from the shelf and placed it on the pan. The dial climbed to seven hundred grams. She removed the block and set it down again. Seven hundred. The zeroing screw had slipped, so the scale added two hundred grams to everything. Mrs. Vale’s basket looked full because the needle had lied; it was short by two hundred grams.

Mrs. Vale returned, and Nella opened her mouth. If she told her now, they could correct the order. If she stayed quiet, Mrs. Vale might never know. Rafi had asked her to work quickly. She had already given Sam an apple from the crate, and the remaining stock was tight. Nella could picture the clinic guests waiting for their tart. She could also picture Mrs. Vale weighing the basket at home and wondering whether Nella had known.

At the adjoining stall, Mrs. Vale stopped to taste a pear slice Nella offered. “Sweeter than I expected,” she said. “If the apples come up short, pears could go in the tart. But I asked for apples.” Nella understood that this was a choice, not permission to quietly change the order. She took a breath. “I need to tell you something before you pay. The scale is adding two hundred grams. I filled this to the mark, but it’s light. I’m sorry.”

Mrs. Vale looked at the iron weight, then at Nella. “You could have let me leave.”

“I nearly did.” Nella kept both hands on the basket so she would not turn it away. “There are enough apples to add one hundred grams. Not two. I can return your money, or you can decide whether the pears belong in the tart.”

Mrs. Vale considered the fruit. “The clinic guests won’t mind a pear. I mind being surprised.” She took another small slice, smiled, and agreed to the blend. Together they added the remaining apples and then enough pear to bring the basket to the exact requested weight. Nella checked the amount on the corrected scale and wrote “apples and pear” on the order slip. Mrs. Vale paid the agreed price. “Thank you for giving me the choice,” she said. Her voice was warmer than before, but not as casual.

Nella did not pretend the work was finished. She turned the small zeroing screw until the empty pan rested at zero, then tested Rafi’s half-kilo weight again. This time it landed exactly on the mark. She wiped apple juice from the pan and carried the scale back to him. He used it immediately to weigh oranges for a customer. “You fixed its use, not just its address,” he said. Nella smiled, though her ears were hot.

At closing, Rafi let her serve the last two customers while he counted the till. Mrs. Vale’s tart was already on its way to the clinic, changed but properly weighed. Nella had lost time and used a pear she had meant to sell, yet she had not made Mrs. Vale pay for a mistake she knew about. The scale stood steady on its hook. When the next order came, Nella began by checking the zero. This time Rafi did not need to remind her.
