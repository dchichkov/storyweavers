## s000029: seed 5029, 7 scenes

Warm afternoon light rested on the greenhouse panes. Mina found Rosa’s seedling tray drooping at the bench edge; the little leaves pointed down like tired fingers. Ivo’s note beside the blue watering can said, “If Rosa’s starts look thirsty, use this. Please bring it back rinsed.” Mina wanted to help before Rosa returned.

Mina lifted the blue can from its rack, careful not to scrape the other tools. The handle wobbled, and she tested its weight with both hands. “I’ll bring it back clean,” she told the quiet greenhouse. The can was still Ivo’s; for now, it was useful in Mina’s hands, not hers to keep.

At the tray, Mina read Rosa’s marker: “Keep evenly moist; don’t flood the new roots.” A spare rubber band held the marker to its stake. Mina slipped it off and kept it beside the label, noticing that the words made the job smaller: water the cells, not the whole bench. She left the marker where Rosa could find it.

The rubber band held the split seam while Mina filled the can. Following the label, she poured into the seedling cells in measured turns. The soil darkened evenly. It had received enough for the roots to drink, though Mina knew water alone would not stop the hot light.

Rosa had not shown Mina the screen, so she did not pull at the rack. Mina slid the tray onto the cool shelf beside the tank, where the wall cast a broad shadow. It was a modest shelter, not a cure, but the leaves no longer sat in the sharpest band of light.

Mina rinsed the blue can at the sink and unwound the rubber band from its spout. She clipped it back to Rosa’s marker before hanging the can on Ivo’s rack. The borrowed tool had carried water where it mattered, and it was clean and ready for the next gardener.

The tray was sheltered, but its thirsty starts were too weak to call saved. Mina pinched two still-firm tips and rooted them in damp compost plugs, writing Rosa’s name beside them. The original tray had not been rescued; something living had. Mina had returned the can and faced the result honestly.
