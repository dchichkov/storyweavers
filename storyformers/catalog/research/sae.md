# SAE and transformer-circuit research for the Storyformer catalog

Research checked on 2026-11-07. This note uses original papers and research reports. The proposed field names and Storyformer functions are design hypotheses; none is claimed to be an identified universal neural feature. The catalog is a reusable language for experiments, not an SAE extracted from a trained model.

## What the research establishes, and what it does not

| Primary source | Relevant result | Consequence for this experiment |
|---|---|---|
| Elhage et al., [Toy Models of Superposition](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/index.html), 2022 | Small controlled networks can represent more sparse features than dimensions, and can compute with superposed features. The report explicitly limits extrapolation from its toy settings. | Dictionary fields deliberately choose an explicit basis. Matching the number of fields to a transformer's width does not establish comparable capacity, compute, or learning behavior. |
| Bricken et al., [Towards Monosemanticity](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features/), 2023 | Dictionary learning extracts more interpretable units than raw neurons in a one-layer transformer. Dictionary size changes the granularity of features. Some features describe contexts; others are better understood through the continuations they promote. | Keep semantic descriptions, context detectors, and next-token preferences distinct. A detector named `verb_context` is not automatically equivalent to the token attribute `pos="v"`. |
| Templeton et al., [Scaling Monosemanticity](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/index.html), 2024 | Large Claude dictionaries include abstract, concrete, multilingual, and multimodal features. Their inventory includes entities, relations, and computational intermediates, but is neither complete nor an ontology specification. Steering supports causal relevance for investigated cases. | Use semantically named families and permit composition. Do not create one field per famous individual, lexical item, or picturesque activation example. Entity identity should normally be a value or reference. |
| Gao et al., [Scaling and evaluating sparse autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04093), 2024 | Top-k SAEs directly control activation sparsity; improved training reduces dead latents. The paper separates reconstruction quality from feature-quality measures and scales to a 16-million-latent GPT-4 dictionary. | A budget such as 64 active fields is a tunable resource constraint. Top-k SAE activations provide no evidence that exactly one of 64 grammar rules should win in a symbolic head. Measure loss, coverage, calibration, and semantic fidelity separately. |
| Chanin et al., [A is for Absorption](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14507), revised 2025 | A general concept can disappear from its nominal feature when a more specific feature represents it. Sparsity encourages this absorption for hierarchical features; changing width or sparsity alone does not solve it. | Missing `is_tool` evidence must not mean `is_tool=false`. A specific `is_cutting_tool` feature should not silently erase an independently declared superclass relation. Absence and negation need separate treatment. |
| Bussmann et al., [Learning Multi-Level Features with Matryoshka Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17547), 2025 | Nested dictionaries encourage general features at small sizes and specialized ones at larger sizes, reducing absorption in the tested models and tasks. | Give catalog entries parent concepts, refinement relations, and aliases. Preserve general fields when adding a more specific detector; evaluate both levels. This is inspiration for organization, not a proof that a symbolic hierarchy will learn well. |
| Paulo et al., [Automatically Interpreting Millions of Features in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13928), 2024 | LLM-generated explanations can be evaluated against held-out activations and interventions. Activation-based explanations and intervention-based explanations need not recover the same aspects of a feature. | An LLM may propose a name, definition, and grammar. Require positive examples, hard negatives, false negatives, and predicted downstream effects before promoting its explanation to a canonical field. |
| Karvonen et al., [SAEBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09532), version 4, 2025 | Its eight evaluation metrics show that better reconstruction/sparsity proxies do not reliably imply better practical interpretability; architectures trade off different desiderata. | Maintain a dashboard rather than a single “interpretability score.” The appropriate metrics for symbolic latents can use known semantic targets directly. |
| Chanin, [Are Sparse Autoencoder Benchmarks Reliable?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18229), 2026 preprint | A recent audit reports substantial metric noise and serious failures for specific benchmark settings, including targeted probe perturbation and spurious-correlation removal. This is an audit of those settings, not a verdict on every causal test. | Use repeatable synthetic interventions with known answers, report denominators, and test whether the metric detects intentionally broken implementations. Avoid accepting tiny score changes without uncertainty estimates. |
| Li et al., [Interpretability Illusions with Sparse Autoencoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16004), 2025 | In the tested scenarios, input perturbations can change SAE concept interpretations substantially while leaving base-model behavior comparatively stable. | Test paraphrases, distractors, names, punctuation, and word order where semantic invariance is intended. Attractive labels on top activations are insufficient validation. |
| Elhage et al., [A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html), 2021 | The framework separates attention's selection behavior from its transported/output effect and studies composition between heads in small attention-only models. | Specify a head's query, key predicate, selected value, output projection, and residual operation separately. “Find the actor” alone is not a full head specification. |
| Olsson et al., [In-context Learning and Induction Heads](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html), 2022 | In the studied small models, a previous-token head and induction head compose to implement repeated-pattern continuation. Evidence for broader large-model explanations is explicitly more indirect. | Include copy, previous-item carrier, repeated-prefix detector, and continuation-retrieval mechanisms. A single unrestricted grammar can collapse the two steps, so its depth/compute is not directly comparable to two neural layers. |
| Wang et al., [Interpretability in the Wild: a Circuit for Indirect Object Identification in GPT-2 small](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00593), 2022 | The studied task uses a circuit of 26 heads in seven classes, evaluated using faithfulness, completeness, and minimality. Roles include moving names and regulating which names get selected. | Entity resolution benefits from producer, selector, and inhibitor functions. Do not equate nearest name with actor, recipient, or antecedent. A correct result in one sentence template is not a complete account of a general head. |
| McDougall et al., [Copy Suppression](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04625), 2023 | A GPT-2 head suppresses some overpredicted context tokens; suppression contributes to calibration and interacts with self-repair after ablation. | Suppression can be useful computation, not merely deletion or failure. Distinguish suppressing a proposed continuation from asserting the negation of a story fact. Single-head ablations may underestimate effects because other paths compensate. |
| Feng and Steinhardt, [How do Language Models Bind Entities in Context?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17191), 2023 | Causal interventions support a binding-ID mechanism connecting entities and attributes in the tested Pythia/LLaMA models. Binding IDs occupy a continuous subspace. | Explicit IDs and typed references are a good symbolic design choice. An attribute must state whose property it is; a flat `afraid=true` without experiencer/scope is ambiguous. This paper does not prescribe the proposed JSON representation. |
| Marks et al., [Sparse Feature Circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.19647), 2024 | Fine-grained feature circuits permit causal analyses and targeted edits that component-level descriptions can obscure. | Trace feature production and consumption, not only head activation counts. Preserve the causal path from source spans through intermediate fields to output distributions. |
| Dunefsky et al., [Transcoders Find Interpretable LLM Feature Circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11944), 2024 | Transcoders approximate MLP input-output computations with sparse features, enabling circuit analysis through MLP layers. | A grammar neuron with tests and an emitted patch is closer to a proposed sparse computation than to an SAE feature alone. Its predicate and projection both need learning and validation. |
| Ameisen et al., [Circuit Tracing: Revealing Computational Graphs in Language Models](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/methods.html), 2025 | Attribution graphs built with a replacement model expose useful mechanisms, but leave reconstruction error, fixed attention patterns, inhibitory behavior, and global generalization unresolved. | Record unmatched and inhibited cases as well as positive activations. Use edits and counterfactual reruns to check mechanisms. A graph from one batch is a hypothesis that needs replay across batches. |
| Lindsey et al., [On the Biology of a Large Language Model](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/attribution-graphs/biology.html), 2025 | Case studies distinguish low-level input features, intermediate concepts, and output-oriented features; report persistent features, delimiter storage, default/override motifs, and multiple paths. | Candidate catalogs should include state carriers, phrase/delimiter summaries, defaults, inhibition, and alternative routes. These observations motivate mechanisms rather than a fixed list of story emotions or tropes. |
| Gurnee et al., [When Models Manipulate Manifolds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04480), archival 2026 version of October 2025 work | The studied linebreaking computation uses count-related feature families with a useful continuous geometric interpretation. Multiple attention heads contribute to the computation. | Quantities should often be numeric fields, not hundreds of boolean thresholds. One symbolic field can summarize a computation that needs several neural features; numerical parity is not architectural parity. |
| Gao et al., [Weight-sparse transformers have interpretable circuits](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/41df8f28-d4ef-43e9-aed2-823f9393e470/circuit-sparsity-paper.pdf), 2025 | Training sparse weights yields more comprehensible circuits on curated tasks while introducing capability/interpretability tradeoffs; preserving interpretability at larger scales remains difficult. | Directly designing sparse symbolic computation is plausible, but it has a capacity cost that must be measured. A large readable catalog alone is not evidence of transformer-equivalent performance. |

## Catalog design rules inferred from that evidence

These are proposed engineering rules for Storyformer, not conclusions measured in the cited papers.

1. **A semantic identity is independent of a detector.** Keep a stable `attribute_id`, a canonical name, a type, a definition, applicability, and evidence policy. Several grammars may produce the same attribute. One grammar may project several mutually coherent fields. Neuron IDs remain stable when display names change.
2. **Separate values from evidence strength.** For example, `belief_content_id` names a proposition; a score says how strongly the residual supports that binding. A probability is not the same as the story character's subjective confidence. The latter, if represented, is a distinct semantic field.
3. **Use references for participants.** `speaker_id`, `experiencer_id`, `goal_owner_id`, and `belief_holder_id` bind to entities. Event/proposition references identify what is feared, promised, believed, or desired. A repeated word string does not establish identity.
4. **Make scope explicit.** Narrator assertion, quotation, belief, dream, plan, negation, hypothetical, and conditional antecedent are different scopes. “Mia thought the door was open” supports a belief attribution; it does not establish that the physical door is open.
5. **Unknown is not false.** Null/absence means not established by this causal prefix. Explicit false needs contrary evidence. Suppressing positive evidence changes a hypothesis score; it does not itself assert a negative fact.
6. **Use types for structure, not decoration.** Boolean predicates are useful for genuinely binary properties; enums for mutually exclusive categories; numbers for quantities; references for identity; arrays/objects for structured claims. Do not allocate separate vocabulary entries for every scalar value or named character merely to reach 1,024 entries.
7. **Permit hierarchy without mandatory firing of every ancestor.** A registry can record that cutting tools are tools. Whether ancestors are materialized in the residual is an implementation decision. Tests of concept absence must consult entailments rather than assume absent fields are negatives.
8. **Names are revisable explanations.** Store `proposed`, `validated_on_slice`, or similar interpretation status. A high-precision name that misses counterexamples can be too broad or absorbed; a lower-level operational name may be more accurate until evidence improves.
9. **Avoid hindsight labels in prefix supervision.** “Set-up,” “foreshadowing,” “betrayal,” and “successful plan” often require later text. Use a visible cue, provisional candidate, unresolved thread, or prediction field until sufficient evidence appears. Record retrospective annotations separately.
10. **Distinguish computation roles.** A noun-context detector, an entity-state copier, and a candidate-word booster can share input tests while having different meanings. Their output fields must make those distinctions visible.

## Proposed semantic families

The following are candidate families for a narrative catalog. They are not a list copied from SAE browsers. Names are illustrative and should be deduplicated against the common registry before use.

| Family | Useful dimensions | Scope/typing constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Entity binding and mention | identity, mention form, referent set, antecedent, discourse status | Entity references; distinguish mention from referent. |
| Participant roles | actor, patient, recipient, instrument, beneficiary, experiencer | Event-relative bindings; lexical class alone cannot assign them. |
| Physical state | location, containment, possession, integrity, openness, mobility | Entity and time anchors; explicit event effects require a separate execution contract. |
| Event organization | type, phase, telicity, completion, result, manner | An event type is not proof that the event happened. |
| Time and aspect | temporal anchor, duration, overlap, succession, frequency | Numeric/relational values; narrator order and world order differ. |
| Epistemic attitude | belief holder, proposition, knowledge claim, doubt, uncertainty, evidence source | Character attitude is distinct from narrator truth. |
| Goals and intentions | goal owner, goal content, commitment, adoption, abandonment, subgoal, obstacle | Plans need explicit or graded evidence, not stereotyped affordances. |
| Affect and appraisal | experiencer, affect kind, target, valence, arousal, controllability, expectedness | Unknown intensity stays null; “danger” does not entail fear. |
| Social relation | trust, obligation, authority, cooperation, rivalry, affiliation, debt | Directed, scoped, possibly disputed; avoid immutable character stereotypes. |
| Communication | speaker, addressee, speech act, proposition, uptake, quotation scope | Reporting, commanding, promising, asking, and believing differ. |
| Discourse organization | topic, focus, contrast, elaboration, causal explanation, correction | Relate discourse units; avoid conflating rhetorical relation with physical causation. |
| Perspective and narrative | focalizer, viewpoint access, temporal framing, unresolved issue, resolution evidence | Prefix-visible state; hindsight interpretations are separately labeled. |
| Predictive control | expected grammatical category, candidate referent, pending argument slot, continuation constraint | Explicitly predictive; do not write a predicted event into committed world state. |
| Computation provenance | supporting spans, source clause, inference rule, scope ID, revision lineage | Metadata rather than story facts; machine-checkable where possible. |

The vocabulary can plausibly grow through meaningful subdimensions within these families. It should not grow by repeating `is_X`, `not_X`, `maybe_X`, and `expects_X` for every lexical X without a distinct semantic and computational need.

## Proposed head functions

Each row is a candidate mechanism, not an assertion that an existing neural head performs the exact story task. The first group has relatively close circuit analogues; later rows are narrative extensions.

| Function | Read/select | Project/operate | Essential negative or intervention case |
|---|---|---|---|
| Previous-item carrier | Immediately preceding latent | Copy a declared field to a predecessor slot | A sentence boundary changes the allowed carry policy. |
| Repeated-prefix continuation | Earlier occurrence of current prefix and its successor | Add evidence for the earlier continuation bundle | Earlier occurrence is in a different scope or uses a different entity. |
| Duplicate mention marker | Same entity ID at multiple positions | Add mention-recurrence evidence | Same surface spelling with distinct IDs. |
| Entity attribute retrieval | A bound entity and its supported property | Copy the value to a current-query field with provenance | A distractor entity has the desired value. |
| Role-sensitive entity mover | Event plus role binding | Copy the appropriate participant ID | Swap actor and recipient while preserving all names. |
| Competing-role inhibitor | Candidate entity violates a role constraint | Suppress that candidate binding | A reflexive construction legitimately permits identity. |
| Copy calibration | Strong continuation preference for a seen token | Suppress unsupported repetition evidence | Repetition is explicitly requested or is the correct continuation. |
| Context carrier | Prior compatible scoped state | Maintain evidence at later positions | A later explicit correction supersedes it. |
| Delimiter summary | Clause or utterance boundary | Carry a bounded context summary to the delimiter | Quoted punctuation must not close the narrator's scope. |
| Default/override gate | Declared default plus contrary evidence | Add default evidence or suppress it on override | Unknown is not enough to claim the opposite default. |
| Relation hop | Entity A linked to B, then B linked to C | Add a typed derived link or candidate | Relations are not generally transitive; require a declared composition. |
| Output preference projector | Semantic fields plus syntactic requirements | Add candidate-token/bundle mass | Same word with an incompatible semantic bundle. |
| Output incompatibility suppressor | Candidate bundle violates a visible constraint | Reduce its support | Low frequency alone does not make a bundle impossible. |
| Quantity comparator | Two explicit numeric quantities | Add less/equal/greater or remaining-amount evidence | Units or reference frames differ. |
| Scope opener | Visible quote, attitude, conditional, or negation construction | Introduce a scope binding | A lexical cue occurs as a quoted word rather than an operator. |
| Scope-preserving copier | Source field and matching scope chain | Copy the field without promoting it to narrator truth | Belief content is copied outside the believer's scope. |
| Evidence-source binder | Perception, report, memory, or inference clause | Bind proposition to its epistemic source | Hearing a claim does not entail accepting it. |
| Belief revision | Same holder/proposition plus explicit correction | Suppress older belief evidence and support the update | Narrator correction without evidence the character learned it. |
| Goal adoption | Explicit goal-taking evidence | Bind goal owner and proposed goal state | Merely mentioning a desirable state. |
| Goal obstacle binder | Event/state blocks an explicitly active goal | Add obstruction relation | A setback for one goal helps a different goal. |
| Goal completion checker | Supported outcome matches a goal criterion | Add completion evidence | Attempt, intention, and imagined success do not complete the goal. |
| Affect attribution | Explicit emotion/appraisal with holder and target | Add scoped affect fields | An observer's label is not necessarily the experiencer's self-report. |
| Social commitment tracker | Promise, agreement, release, or refusal | Bind obligation and commitment status | A request alone does not create an accepted promise. |
| Dialogue turn binder | Speaker, addressee, quote boundaries | Maintain utterance participation | A character names someone other than the addressee. |
| Question expectation | Interrogative act and queried slot | Add expected answer-type evidence | A rhetorical question may not solicit an answer. |
| Repair/correction integrator | Correction target plus replacement content | Add revision relation; suppress outdated hypothesis | Negating a quoted claim without supplying a replacement. |
| Contrast resolver | Two discourse units with a contrast cue | Bind contrasted dimensions | The conjunction “but” need not denote physical opposition. |
| Causal claim binder | Explicit causal construction | Record a scoped cause claim | Mere temporal succession or “after” is not sufficient causation. |
| Viewpoint carrier | Focalizer and access scope | Carry whose perceptions/knowledge constrain the passage | Omniscient narration does not share one character's ignorance. |
| Unresolved-thread tracker | Introduced problem/goal/question without visible closure | Maintain an open issue | A topic shift is not automatically a resolution. |
| Resolution evidence binder | Outcome explicitly closes an earlier issue | Add relation between issue and outcome | A locally positive event leaves the original issue open. |
| Interpretation disagreement marker | Multiple compatible typed readings | Preserve alternatives or flag ambiguity | Do not force one reading simply because one rule has a nicer name. |

These functions can use several neurons each. Conversely, one carefully bounded neuron can implement a narrow instance of a function. Neither direction implies one catalog row must occupy one physical head slot.

## Grammar proposal and naming protocol

For a candidate neuron, the learner should preserve: its input predicate; captured variables; emitted typed fields; residual operation; conditional support; weighted prediction loss effect; positive examples; hardest matching counterexamples; hardest expected-but-unmatched examples; and downstream consumers. The LLM sees a compact batch packet plus historical summaries and proposes a structured change. Replay checks the change across prior batches, including stale naming conventions.

For example, suppose a learned predicate fires on a current verb and emits an opaque boolean. A proposed rename to `pos="v"` is justified only if its observed predicate agrees with the existing POS convention and its consumers expect the token's POS. If it instead fires after a verb, or only before a missing object, the better field may be `pending_patient_slot` or another operational hypothesis. The name must describe the field's semantics at its write position, not the most conspicuous word in the examples.

At minimum, evaluate:

- **Semantic precision and recall:** known positive/negative labels, stratified by scope and construction; unknown labels excluded from false counts.
- **Selective effects:** change the intended participant/property and check which outputs change; unrelated bindings should remain stable.
- **Paraphrase and renaming:** preserve meaning while changing lexical forms, order where licensed, names, and distractors.
- **Inhibition:** distinguish no match, a matched NOOP, suppression, explicit false, and a failed type/scope check.
- **Compositional holdouts:** train on individual properties and test unfamiliar combinations and longer dependency spans.
- **Replay and versioning:** retain historical cases; reset or replay match-derived statistics when predicates change.
- **Prediction fidelity:** score the full next-latent distribution before top-k display truncation, including semantic bundle alternatives.
- **Structural cost:** matching states, support count, emitted fields, memory, and latency; a readable but unbounded search is not cheap.

The target of 1,024 attributes, 1,024 heads, and 1,024 grammars is useful as a broad hypothesis inventory. No reviewed source establishes 1,024 as a sufficient ontology, 64 as the right active-field budget, or a fixed correspondence between grammar counts and transformer parameters.

## Fit to the current local runtime

Inspected `storyformers/grammar.py`, `transformer.py`, and `neuron_spec.py`. Current executable grammar support includes finite sequence/alternative/repetition patterns, full-context matching with explicit gaps, equality tests, `Present`, `OneOf`, captures, field references, and declared KV lookup. Heads add or suppress evidence on the **current query only**, all heads within a layer read the same snapshot, and subsequent layers can consume the newly added fields. Final projection produces complete candidate bundles.

That supports useful bounded witnesses for copying, typing, scoped transfer, finite pattern completion, and explicit event-role binding. It does not yet provide arithmetic, general graph traversal, dynamically allocating arbitrary proposition/scope graphs, resolving all competing antecedents, learned top-one routing across 64 neurons, or a general rule for committing physical effects. Catalog contracts needing those capabilities should say `adapter_required`, and explain the required adapter rather than pretend a lexical trigger implements the operation.

The runtime's additive score is evidence about a field value. A `suppress` head lowers that evidence; it does not remove the token, mutate earlier latents, or turn `true` into `false`. This is compatible with uncertainty-aware semantic fields if the catalog definitions preserve the distinction.

Cross-review also found a concrete limit on multi-field semantic writes. Two matched belief sources `(content=p_zebra, event=ev_a)` and `(content=p_apple, event=ev_b)` with the same holder and scope produce a flattened residual view `(content=p_apple, event=ev_a)` under equal scores: that tuple appeared in neither source. The observed `assertion_status=asserted` also remains above a newly added `candidate` score. A matching dictionary is therefore not automatically a correlated residual hypothesis. All 64 finite mental-domain matcher seeds are retained inside `contract.seed_matcher`, but their complete heads are classified `adapter_required`. The adapter must route a whole holder/value/event/scope candidate with its status inside an atomic envelope; it must not rewrite the token's observed status or treat a historical source event as the query's current event. The remaining 192 mental-domain contracts already require semantic adapters. This yields 256 adapter contracts with 64 executable finite seeds, not 64 safely native mental heads.

Finally, Storyweavers' physical-carrier principle remains a separate execution invariant: a discourse feature about fear, a predicted plan, or a grammatical expectation is not an enacted physical story event. Any future bridge to the world model should demand explicit entity/event binding and an authorized state transition.
