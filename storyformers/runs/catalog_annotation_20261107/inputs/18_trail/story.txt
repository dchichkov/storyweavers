At the entrance to Brackenwood Discovery Trail, Ranger Ivo placed a brass compass in Nia’s palm.

“Borrow it, don’t lose it,” he said. “The red end points north. Keep the map turned the same way as the compass, and you won’t mistake a bend for a new trail.”

Nia had been practicing with her grandfather’s old maps. She wanted to prove she could lead a hike without asking for help. Felix wanted the last copper-acorn stamp on his discovery card; then he’d have every station. Their friend Leni had a different mission: she hoped to sketch a spotted salamander near the stream.

She moved carefully on crutches, her ankle still healing. She’d told them she could manage the trail. Still, Nia and Felix promised to keep beside her.

The three set off beneath dripping beech leaves. At each station, Felix read a clue, Nia checked the map, and Leni noticed things they might otherwise have passed: a snail’s silver trail, a feather caught in a fern, a round hole where a woodpecker had searched for breakfast.

By the time they reached the fork, the clouds had turned the sky the color of pewter. The sign pointed to the Pine Knob Shortcut, fifteen minutes, or the Fern Loop, thirty-five. On the map, the shortcut climbed steep, uneven steps. The loop curved gently along the stream.

Felix checked his watch. “The stamp table closes at five. We’ve got forty minutes.”

“If we take the shortcut, we’ll make it,” Nia said. Then she looked at the wet wooden steps. Leni’s crutch tips rested at the edge of the path.

Leni looked from the shortcut to the loop. “I can do the Fern Loop,” she said. “I just can’t do it at a sprint.”

Felix sighed. He had been polishing his discovery card all morning. “We might miss the stamp.”

“Then we might miss it,” Nia said. “We’re not leaving you at the fork.”

Leni smiled, but she pointed to the map. “The loop is easier. It’s also the way to the salamander pool.”

They turned onto the Fern Loop.

Rain began pattering through the canopy. The path darkened, and a puddle spread across it, reflecting a bent branch like a black antler. Nia held the map beneath her jacket while Felix guarded the compass from the rain.

Around the next bend, a fallen birch blocked the trail. Its pale trunk lay across the path, its branches tangled in the bushes.

“We can climb over,” Felix said, looking at the trunk.

Leni tested the muddy ground with one crutch. It slid a little. “Not safely.”

Nia studied the map. A dotted line marked a short detour, but the sign that should point to it was hidden behind dripping leaves. She opened her palm compass and turned slowly until the red needle settled north. Then she rotated the map to match.

“The stream should be on our left,” she said. “If we go back to the last blue marker, we can look for the detour.”

They retraced their steps, slowly enough for Leni to keep her balance. Felix held the compass while Nia checked the map. At the blue marker, Leni lifted her chin.

“There,” she said. A blue triangle had been painted on a tree trunk, almost covered by wet ivy. Beyond it, a narrow but level path curved around the fallen birch.

Felix grinned. “Good eyes.”

“Good map,” Nia said, and gave Leni the compass to hold while she pushed the ivy aside.

The detour brought them to the stream. Rainwater trickled over the stones, and beneath a flat, mossy log, a spotted salamander paused with its bright orange dots shining against its dark back.

Leni stopped. Felix stopped. Nia stopped too.

“Don’t get too close,” Leni whispered. She opened her sketchbook and drew quickly, while the salamander slipped into the leaves. The rain tapped the log like tiny fingers.

When they reached the stamp table, Ranger Ivo was stacking the cards. “Just in time,” he said.

Felix held out his discovery card. The copper acorn stamp landed in its final empty square. Nia returned the compass, its brass cover still dry beneath Felix’s jacket. Leni showed Ranger Ivo her drawing, and he leaned closer to admire the orange spots.

Outside the gate, the rain had stopped. A puddle beside the trail sign held three wavering reflections: Nia with the map, Felix with his stamped card, and Leni balancing on her crutches, sketchbook open to a small salamander curled beneath a painted blue triangle.
