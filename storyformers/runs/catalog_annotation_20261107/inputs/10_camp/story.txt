The last strip of sunlight lay across the picnic table at Cedar Bend Campground. Nora was helping her mother stir beans in a pot when Theo came running from the tent, one shoe untied and his cheeks wet.

“Moss is gone,” he said.

Moss was his small brown rabbit, with one floppy ear and a brass bell sewn to its blue collar. Theo took Moss everywhere—even to brush his teeth.

“Where did you last see him?” Nora asked.

“At the water pump. I had him, and then I didn’t.” Theo looked toward the trees. Their long shadows reached across the campground loop. “I can’t go look. The woods are getting dark.”

Nora wanted to find Moss before bedtime. She also wanted Theo to stop looking as if the shadows might swallow him. “We’ll look together,” she said.

Their father was hanging a towel on the clothesline. Nora asked if she could borrow his lantern.

“Stay on the marked path,” he said, handing it over. “And come back when Mom calls for supper.”

Nora clicked the lantern on. Its warm circle of light landed on the dirt. Theo stood close enough that his shoulder pressed against hers.

They checked beneath the table and around the tent. Nora lifted a folding chair. Theo peered behind the cooler.

“Not there,” he said.

“Let’s retrace your steps to the pump.”

The path curved between tall pines. Nora walked slowly so Theo could keep up. The lantern made the tree trunks bright on one side and black on the other. A branch swayed overhead, and its shadow slid across the path.

Theo grabbed Nora’s sleeve. “Something moved.”

“Just the branch,” she said, though her own stomach had tightened. She aimed the lantern upward. The branch waved in the breeze, then held still.

They reached the water pump. No rabbit. Nora checked the wooden platform while Theo looked around the spigot.

“I should’ve held him tighter,” he whispered.

“You were carrying him and washing your hands. That’s a lot of jobs.”

Theo sniffed. “I heard his bell when we came back. I think.”

They followed the path toward camp. Ahead, it split: one trail curved past the bridge over the shallow creek; the other turned toward the campground office.

“We could check the office,” Nora said. “Maybe someone found him.”

Theo stared at the bridge. Beyond it, the path dipped between dark shrubs.

“I don’t want to go there.”

Nora wanted to hurry. If Moss had rolled down the trail, every minute mattered. She could carry the lantern and go ahead—but Theo would be alone at the fork, with the shadows and no light.

“Okay,” she said. “We’ll stay together. You tell me what you remember.”

Theo took a breath. “I was running back from the pump. I heard a tiny jingle, right near the bridge.”

They walked toward it side by side. At the railing, Nora swept the lantern over the boards. The light caught a jumble of leaves, then the silver shine of creek water below.

Theo leaned close to her. “There!”

A blue patch showed beneath a low pine branch beside the path. Moss had caught by one floppy ear, just above the ground. His bell made a faint sound as the branch shifted.

Nora could reach him, but the branch stretched over a tangle of prickly blackberry canes. Theo looked at the dark tangle and backed away.

“Can you hold the lantern steady?” Nora asked. “I’ll reach from the path. Don’t move closer.”

Theo swallowed. Then he took the lantern with both hands. The light wobbled once, painting a tall, crooked shadow over the bridge.

“It’s only the branch,” Nora reminded him.

Theo aimed the lantern lower. The shadow shrank. “I can see his ear!”

Nora crouched and stretched beneath the pine. A thorn snagged her sleeve, but she worked it free and caught Moss by the blue collar. She pulled gently. The rabbit came loose, leaves stuck to his fur and his bell giving one bright chime.

Theo hugged him to his chest. “You found him!”

“We found him,” Nora said, rubbing the thorn mark on her sleeve.

From the campsite, their mother called, “Supper!”

They returned along the marked path, with Theo carrying Moss and Nora carrying the lantern. When they reached the picnic table, their father held out a hand for it. Nora gave it back.

Theo sat beside the warm pot and set Moss on the bench. He smoothed the rabbit’s bent ear. Behind them, the lantern’s glow made three shadows on the tent wall: Theo’s, Nora’s, and a small one with one floppy ear. This time, Theo leaned back and let them all stay.
