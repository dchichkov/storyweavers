# Unfiltered sample positions

## Seed 1000

On Thursday, the music room filled with wandering piano scales and sleepy flute notes; Nia noticed the little drum on the shelf; its red ribbon hung from the handle. Then she wanted a duet for Saturday with a drumbeat and an answering part. Then Dalia lent Nia the drum until Saturday, its red ribbon knotted around the handle. Then Nia asked Mateo if he might play the answering part with her at the picnic. Mateo said that he liked playing, but did not want a roomful of eyes on him yet. Nia practiced the drum pattern on the rug, leaving little pauses between the beats, the red ribbon bobbing with each stroke. Mateo tapped the same rhythm on his knees; she noticed the answer without treating it as permission. Dalia set the signup sheet beside the piano, announcing that one act remained for Saturday. Nia wrote only her own name on the signup sheet, choosing a drum solo rather than listing Mateo without his consent. On Saturday, folding chairs crowded the room as Dalia introduced the solo; she played a steady pattern that opened into a pause, then filled the pause with quieter beats. She asked Mateo whether he wanted to try the answering part privately on the rug after the crowd thinned. Mateo agreed to try a private duet on the rug, choosing the answering part for himself. Mateo crossed to the shelf and picked up the second drum to carry it to the rug. Mateo joined Nia on the rug with one testing answer beat; Nia left room for two more. Mateo returned the second drum to the shelf, both instruments back where they belonged. Nia returned the borrowed drum to the shelf, the red ribbon settling against the wooden rim. The two drums rested side by side on the shelf, their unlisted duet fading beneath the room quiet sounds.

## Seed 1001

Then Nia wrote only her own name on the signup sheet, choosing a drum solo and leaving the public choice with Mateo. Before the waiting rows of chairs, Dalia introduced Nia drum solo; she played two quick beats and a wide pause, then filled the pause with quieter beats. She asked Mateo whether he wanted to try the answering part privately away from the program. Mateo said he would rather listen than play; she left the choice with him without asking again. Nia returned the borrowed drum to the shelf, the red ribbon settling against the wooden rim. The borrowed drum rested beside the untouched second drum on the shelf; the program still named Nia alone, and Mateo choice stayed his.
