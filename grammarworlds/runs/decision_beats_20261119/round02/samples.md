## pottery: appraise_usefulness → keep_dent

The Cup in the Drawing

On Saturday, Bea would surprise Grandpa Len with a birthday cup. It had to be straight and smooth, like the drawing. But the shaping rib slipped. A dent appeared below the handle of the cup. Bea hid the dent behind the sponge. Bea said, “I could start again.” Mr Ivo said, “You can. But a new cup won't be ready for Saturday.” When Grandpa Len arrived, Bea brought out the cup, with its dent facing him. Bea said, “Your present has gone wrong. Will you help me?” Grandpa Len held his stiff hand beside the cup. His fingers needed more room than his old mug allowed. Grandpa Len said, “My thumb needs somewhere here.” Bea said, “Where the dent is?” Bea had been hiding the dent from Grandpa Len. Now she wondered whether it could help him hold the cup. Bea looked at her drawing, then put it away. With the sponge, she rounded the dent into a place for his thumb. By Saturday, Mr Ivo had finished firing and cooling the cup. Bea set the cup on Grandpa Len's table with the dent facing him. Grandpa Len filled the cup with tea. With his thumb in the hollow, he lifted it in one hand.

The End.

The Answering Beat

On the concert sheet, Nia wrote Nia and Mateo. Beside them she drew two little drums. She wanted an answering beat at the concert. Nia tapped three quick beats. Mateo answered with two slow ones. Dalia opened the practice room door. Beyond it, chairs stood in rows. Mateo put his sticks down. Mateo said, “Take my name off.” Nia said, “We practiced all week.” Mateo said, “I want to play with you. I do not want everyone looking.” Dalia said, “You can play alone. But I need the final list now.” Nia looked at the sticks Mateo had set down. Without his answer, the concert turn seemed less important. Nia said, “What if it is just us, after the concert?” Mateo said, “With the door shut?” Nia said, “Shut.” Mateo picked up his sticks again. With the pencil, Nia struck out both names. Dalia gave their turn to the next child. After the last applause, Nia and Mateo moved their drums together in the practice room. Nia shut the practice room door. Nia tapped three quick beats. Two slow beats answered. Behind the closed door, neither drum was quiet for long.

The End.

One Can Left

Nia tied the yellow ribbon around the sunflower pot for the show at two. The show trolley waited by the door. The tomato seedlings drooped in their tray inside the greenhouse. One leaf had already curled. The broken tap gave no water. Mr Bell said, “One can left in the barrel. No repair part until tomorrow.” Mr Bell handed over the watering can. Nia emptied the barrel into it. Mr Bell said, “The little class planted those.” Nia said, “Will half a can be enough?” Mr Bell said, “It may be enough, with shade. You must stay to keep moving the umbrella.” Mr Bell said, “Their bus is due. I am going with them on the afternoon trip.” Nia looked from her sunflower to the little class's seedlings. Sharing might help both, but she would have to stay. Nia looked at the trolley, then poured half the water over the seedlings and half into the sunflower pot. The torn shade cloth slipped from its hook. Sunlight reached the tray. Nia opened her umbrella above the tray. Its handle slid sideways. Nia said, “Hold it while I tie it.” Mr Bell caught the handle and held it straight. Nia tied the handle to the heavy pot. Together they moved the flower beside the tray. Nia returned the empty can. Mr Bell hurried to the school gate to meet the bus. At two, Nia was still turning the umbrella to follow the shade. The show had begun without her. The trolley stood unused. Under the umbrella, the yellow ribbon brushed the lifted seedling leaves.

The End.
