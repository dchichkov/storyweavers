# Sampled titles, openings and closings

Eight fixed plot-audit narratives, presented with the new weighted grammar. Body prose, native events and world state are preserved. Titles, openings and closings have their own annotated-word distributions.

## adapt_gift, seed 7

A Gift for Grandpa Len

Nia told Omar, “I want to give Grandpa Len a cup.” The cup's handle was wide because Grandpa Len's hand was stiff. The shaping rib slipped. The cup was dented. Omar said, “The cup's wall is thick.” Grandpa Len said, “I could hold a bowl with two hands.” Nia told Omar, “I would like a thumb rest.” With the sponge, Nia formed the dent into a thumb rest. Omar fired the cup twice. The cup was cool. Nia handed Grandpa Len the cup. Grandpa Len poured tea into the cup. Grandpa Len's thumb rested in the thumb rest.

The End.

Styles: title = gift, opening = immediate, closing = the_end.

## changed_approach, seed 103

Nia and the Shadow

One day, Nia was at the workshop.

Nia said, “I would like to show Dot a small shadow.” Omar told Nia, “I want a dragon.” Nia borrowed the torch. Nia moved the torch. The shadow grew. Omar read the instruction label. Nia said, “I need to steady the torch.” Nia steadied the torch with the clay wedge. Nia moved the paper moth toward the screen. The shadow shrank. Dot said, “I could try.” Dot held the paper moth beside the screen. The shadow was small.

Styles: title = hero_and_object, opening = everyday, closing = final_image.

## Behavior and limits

- The title appears once, followed by an optional opening, the intact story, and an optional closing.
- Immediate openings and final-image closings reuse the existing first/last scene; they do not duplicate it.
- Incomplete plots offer “To be continued” forms. Empty generations get no invented wrapper story.
- Openings use initial identity/location; titles can name the final object, including a cup reshaped into a bowl.
- No new weather, habits, dialogue, future happiness or physical outcomes are invented.
- This improves presentation; it does not establish a stronger plot or a higher literary-quality score.
