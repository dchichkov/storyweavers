## pottery, seed 103

Bea and the Cup

Once upon a time, there was someone named Bea.

Bea said, “I want to give a cup to Grandpa Len.” The cup's handle was wide because Grandpa Len's hand was stiff. The shaping rib slipped. The cup was dented. Bea hesitated. Mr Ivo told Bea, “The cup's wall is thick.” Grandpa Len said, “I could hold a bowl with two hands.” Bea said, “I would like to smooth the dent.” With the sponge, Bea smoothed out the dent. Mr Ivo fired the cup twice. The cup was cool. Bea handed the cup to Grandpa Len. Grandpa Len poured tea into the cup. Grandpa Len held the cup's wide handle.

## repair, seed 103

Ada and the Blue Cart

One day, Ada was at the courtyard.

The blue cart was rattling. Ada said, “I want to repair the blue cart.” Niko said, “I need to move the tomato tray.” Ada borrowed the spanner. With the spanner, she tightened the nut. The wheel was locked. Ada looked closely at the wheel. Ada said, “I am wrong.” Niko placed the tomato tray under the bench. She loosened the nut. Ada discovered the kite string inside the wheel. Niko held the wheel. She unwound the kite string. She fitted the wheel. The wheel spun smoothly. The blue cart no longer rattled. Ada and Niko placed the tomato tray in the blue cart. Ada pushed the blue cart to the sheltered corner. She returned the spanner to the bench. The tomato tray was sheltered. The wheel was quiet.

Styles: title = hero_and_object, opening = everyday, closing = final_image.

## museum, seed 103

Miri and the Shadow

One day, Miri was at the museum.

Miri said, “I want to show Dot a small shadow.” Kit said, “I would like a dragon.” Miri borrowed the torch. Miri moved the torch. The shadow grew. Miri hesitated. Kit read the instruction label. Miri told Kit, “I need to steady the torch.” Miri steadied the torch with the clay wedge. Miri moved the paper moth toward the screen. The shadow shrank. Dot told Miri, “I can try.” This was the small shadow Miri had wanted. Dot held the paper moth beside the screen. The shadow was small.

## Behavior and limits

- The title appears once, followed by an optional opening, the intact story, and an optional closing.
- Immediate openings and final-image closings reuse the existing first/last scene; they do not duplicate it.
- Incomplete plots offer “To be continued” forms. Empty generations get no invented wrapper story.
- Standalone source completion uses its native ending flag, separately from executable plot achievement; final-image reuse needs an authored image role.
- Openings use initial identity/location; titles can name the final object, including a cup reshaped into a bowl.
- No new weather, habits, dialogue, future happiness or physical outcomes are invented.
- This improves presentation; it does not establish a stronger plot or a higher literary-quality score.
