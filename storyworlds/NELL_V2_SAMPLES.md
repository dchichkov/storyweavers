At half past four, a dragon arrived at Nell's window. She was reading.

"Will you help me get my emerald back?" the dragon asked.

"Where is it?"

They went to a narrow pine. A magpie had the pea-sized emerald in her nest. The dragon could reach the nest; his great claw could not fit inside without breaking its branches.

Nell asked how the jewel had reached the tree.

"She lifted it from my windowsill. I hadn't given it to her," the dragon said.

"We'll try to get it back without breaking her home."

The dragon offered a ruby the size of a cabbage. It was far too big for the magpie to carry.

With the dragon so close, the magpie would not come down.

"Surely this is enough," the dragon said.

"It needs to be small enough for her to use."

The magpie turned the emerald toward the sun. Nell watched the green light catch along its edge.

Nell found a brass button in her pocket.

It was a bright little button, just the right size for a small beak.

"Move back, close your mouth, and be quiet," Nell said.

"All three?"

"All three."

He moved back from the tree, closed his mouth, and stopped making noise. The magpie could approach without passing his teeth.

Nell set the tin dish on the ground and laid the brass button inside it.

The magpie came down, inspected the brass button, and picked it up in her beak.

The bird flew back to the nest with the brass button in her beak. She had not moved the emerald yet.

The button took the jewel's place on the narrow display ledge. Making room for it, the bird pushed the emerald out of the nest.

Something green fell through the branches.

The dragon caught the emerald before it reached the grass. It rested safely in his great claw.

With the matter settled, they set off along the homeward path.

"How can I thank you?" the dragon asked.

"Tell me a true story about somewhere you've been."

"I will."

"I'll ask questions."

The button shone in the nest. The emerald rested in the dragon's claw, and Nell was already choosing her first question.

"It was on my windowsill. I saw her carry it away, but I hadn't given it to her," the dragon said.

"Then we know how it got here."

He opened his claw to offer a cabbage-sized ruby. Nell compared it with the little bird: she could never lift it.

With the dragon so close, the magpie would not come down.

"It is worth a kingdom," the dragon said.

"She hasn't got a kingdom-sized pocket."

The ruby was magnificently unhelpful.

The bird adjusted the emerald on the nest's rim until it shone. Nell noticed that she was arranging a decoration.

"She's decorating," Nell said.

"I hadn't thought of myself as a supplier."

A brass button turned up when Nell checked her pocket.

It was a bright little button, just the right size for a small beak.

"She needs room to come down," Nell said.

"How much room?"

"More than your teeth are giving her."

The dragon stepped back, shut his mouth, and fell quiet. The space beneath the nest was clear.

Being quiet took up none of the clearing, which was an improvement.

The brass button went into the little tin dish, where the magpie could inspect it.

"Should I explain its value?" the dragon asked.

"Let's try letting it be useful."

The bird landed beside the dish. After a close look, she took the brass button.

"She likes it," the dragon said.

"Yes. Keep waiting."

The magpie carried the brass button up to the nest. The emerald was still there.

There was room for only one ornament on the little ledge. The magpie put the button there and nudged the emerald over the edge.

Something green fell through the branches.

The dragon caught the emerald before it reached the grass. It rested safely in his great claw.

"She chose a button," the dragon said.

"It was her choice to make."

With the matter settled, they set off along the homeward path.

"May I repay you with a story?" the dragon asked.

"A true one. From your travels."

"You do make conditions."

"And ask questions."

"A true story, then. I promise."

The button shone in the nest. The emerald rested in the dragon's claw, and Nell was already choosing her first question.

The clock had just struck half past four when the dragon came to Nell's window. Her book lay open.

"Can you rescue a treasure?" the dragon asked.

"Does it need rescuing?"

"I was hoping you wouldn't start there."

The pea-sized emerald was in a magpie's nest, high in a narrow pine. The dragon's claw could reach it, but would break the branches.

A twig shifted at the nest's edge. The rim sagged until it rested on the emerald; Nell saw what was carrying its weight.

"That's a prop, not just a treasure," Nell said.

"A very expensive prop."

Before calling anyone a thief, Nell wanted to know where the jewel had been.

"It was on my windowsill. I saw her carry it away, but I hadn't given it to her," the dragon said.

"Then we know how it got here."

The dragon offered a ruby the size of a cabbage. It was far too big for the magpie to carry.

The magpie stayed among the branches, away from his mouth.

"Look how it sparkles!" the dragon said.

"She would need a cart to take it home."

The ruby was magnificently unhelpful.

A small wooden brace could hold the rim up. Nell had a stick and cord in her pocket, but still had to put them together.

Nell tied the cord to both ends of the stick, making a little brace that could link the loose rim to a sturdy branch.

"That is a rather small piece of engineering," the dragon said.

"It is a rather small nest."

"She needs room to come down," Nell said.

"How much room?"

"More than your teeth are giving her."

The dragon stepped back, shut his mouth, and fell quiet. The space beneath the nest was clear.

Being quiet took up none of the clearing, which was an improvement.

"We need your strength, not your claws inside the nest," Nell said.

"That is a useful distinction."

He supported the thick branch from below, with his mouth still well away. The loose rim rested steadily while they prepared to repair it.

Nell set the tin dish on the ground and laid the wooden brace inside it.

"Should I explain its value?" the dragon asked.

"Let's try letting it be useful."

The bird landed beside the dish. After a close look, she took the wooden brace.

"Well, that's settled," the dragon said.

"Not yet. Watch what she does."

The bird flew back to the nest with the wooden brace in her beak. She had not moved the emerald yet.

With the branch steady, the bird worked the brace into place. Its cord joined the loose rim to a firm branch, giving the nest a new support.

The new brace took the stone's old job. The bird eased the emerald out, flew down, and laid it in the tin dish.

He picked up the returned stone. The green jewel lay safely in his free claw.

"May I have my other hand back?" the dragon asked.

"A little at a time."

He lowered his supporting hand a little, then took it away. The brace held the rim steady on its own.

The dragon walked home beside Nell, leaving the magpie in her tree.

"I owe you something," the dragon said.

"Tell me a true story about a place you've visited."

"May I be splendid in it?"

"Only where you really were."

"Agreed. One true story."

The repaired rim stayed level as they left. The dragon's emerald rested in his claw, and his other hand was free.

### QA

**Why did the dragon need help?**

The emerald was in a magpie's nest in a narrow pine. His claw could reach it, but taking it that way would break the branches.

**What was the jewel doing in the nest?**

It was supporting a loose section of the nest's rim. Nell saw how the loose rim rested on the stone.

**How had the jewel reached the nest?**

The dragon saw the magpie take it from his windowsill. He told Nell that he had not given it to her.

**Why did the ruby offer fail?**

The ruby was cabbage-sized, too big and heavy for the magpie to carry. Its value did not make it usable by such a small bird.

**How was the brace made?**

Nell attached soft cord to both ends of a short straight stick. The brace could then be tied between the loose rim and a sturdy branch.

**How did the dragon make it safe for the magpie to approach?**

He moved back, closed his mouth, and became quiet. The bird no longer had to pass close to his teeth to reach the offer.

**What did the dragon do during the nest repair?**

He steadied the thick branch from underneath while keeping his head back. His temporary support kept the nest still while the permanent brace was fitted.

**Why could the magpie remove the jewel safely?**

The fitted brace had taken over its job of supporting the loose rim. The magpie could pull the stone free and carry it to the dish without letting the nest sag.

**How did they know the repair was finished?**

The dragon slowly withdrew his temporary support. The brace held the nest's rim steady without either his hand or the jewel beneath it.

**What did the dragon promise in return for the help?**

He promised Nell a true story about his travels. She said she would ask questions; the telling was still ahead of them.
