# Dialogue-Heavy StoryWorld Samples

Three hand-authored, standalone examples. Conversation carries misunderstandings, objections, information, and agreements.
These are small reference generators, not a claim of thousands of independent plots. Each has three core problems and two conversation approaches.
The examples below use fixed names and parameters. All execution is local; no generation or judge API was used.

## Library Words

Source: [library_words_dialogue.py](worlds/library_words_dialogue.py)

```bash
./.venv/bin/python storyworlds/worlds/library_words_dialogue.py --problem place --approach guess --hero Mia --friend Ben --item boat --qa
```

Mia and Ben wanted to finish The Moon Boat before walking home. Two small chairs waited beside the library's reading table.

"Will you save my place while I fill my water bottle?" Mia asked.

"Of course. Nobody else can have it," Ben said.

Mia went to the fountain. Ben closed the book and spread both arms across the empty chair.

"I'm back! Where were we?" Mia asked.

"Right here. I kept your chair," Ben said.

"You weren't listening. Now we've lost it," Mia blurted.

"I was listening. I've been guarding this chair the whole time," Ben said.

"Oh. What did you think I wanted you to save?" Mia asked.

"Your place to sit. The chair, not the page," Ben said.

"I meant the place in our story. I didn't say that bit," Mia said.

"I can help find it. What do you remember?" Ben asked.

"There was a silver sail. I wanted to see what happened next," Mia said.

"Then let's look for a silver sail, not guess a page number," Ben said.

"I'll turn slowly. You tell me when you see it," Mia said.

The pages rustled. Ben tapped the right picture, and Mia slid a paper strip beside it.

"This is the one. The bookmark can guard it now," Ben said.

"Will you keep my place again? My chair this time," Mia said.

"Chair for me, page for the bookmark. That's a job I understand," Ben said.

The water bottle stood between the chairs. One paper corner peeked from the book, keeping a much smaller place.

### Story QA

**Why did the readers lose their page?**

Mia asked for a place to be saved, but Ben understood that to mean the chair. Ben closed the book without marking the page.

**How did they find and keep their reading place?**

Mia described a silver sail, and Ben used that detail to find their reading place. They put a paper bookmark at that page before reading on.

**What did the friends learn to say more clearly?**

The same word had meant a chair to one friend and a page to the other. They named the chair and the page separately when asking for help.

## One Cart, Two Plans

Source: [one_cart_dialogue.py](worlds/one_cart_dialogue.py)

```bash
./.venv/bin/python storyworlds/worlds/one_cart_dialogue.py --problem gate --approach guess --hero Mia --friend Ben --item beans --qa
```

Mia held a tray of bean seedlings. Ben had a box of wooden blocks. Between them stood the only cart in the garden, with one shiny red handle.

"I need the cart," Mia said.

"So do I. One handle, two hands wanting it," Ben said.

"The plants need the shady bench," Mia said.

"The blocks need the play mat. We're going different ways," Ben said.

"Let's load both things. One trip, then we're finished," Mia said.

"Have you looked at the little gate?" Ben asked.

"The cart will fit. It's only a small cart," Mia said.

Mia rolled the empty cart up to the gate. Its wheels stopped against the posts.

"Small cart. Smaller gate," Ben said.

"The cart is wider than the opening. Loading it won't change that," Ben said.

"Could we turn it sideways?" Mia asked.

"Then the tray would tip. I'd rather keep the plants inside their pots," Ben said.

"I wanted to solve this with the cart," Mia said.

"Do we need the cart, or do we need the things on the other side?" Ben asked.

"The things. I suppose the cart hasn't asked to come," Mia said.

"We can carry the tray together. Then we can bring the blocks," Ben said.

"Let's park the cart and carry one small load at a time, together," Mia said.

"Agreed. You take that handle; I'll take this one," Ben said.

They parked the cart beside the gate and stood at opposite ends of the tray.

They carried the tray level through the gate and set it on the shady bench.

"Not a single pot tipped," Mia said.

"Now the box. Same team, different handles," Ben said.

Together they brought the small block box through and lowered it onto the play mat.

"The cart can watch us build," Mia said.

"It's very good at staying on its own side of the gate," Ben said.

The cart waited outside. Inside, a green tray and a wooden town stood on opposite sides of two dusty footprints.

### Story QA

**Why did they stop trying to use the cart?**

The cart's wheels were wider than the gate opening. The friends agreed to carry the loads through instead of tipping or forcing the cart.

**How did both loads get through the narrow gate?**

The friends carried the tray together, keeping it level. They then returned for the block box and carried that through together too.

**Where was the cart when they finished?**

They parked the cart beside the gate because it could not fit through. It stayed there while the friends carried both loads into the garden.

## Bridge Builders

Source: [bridge_builders_dialogue.py](worlds/bridge_builders_dialogue.py)

```bash
./.venv/bin/python storyworlds/worlds/bridge_builders_dialogue.py --problem short --approach guess --hero Mia --friend Ben --item bus --qa
```

Mia balanced a tiny paper parcel on a red bus. Ben had built a station on a box across a pretend river. Their cardboard bridge was the last piece of the route.

"One delivery for your station. Is the road ready?" Mia asked.

"It looks like a bridge. I'm not sure it works like one yet," Ben said.

"I'll give it a good push," Mia said.

The front wheels reached the end of the strip before reaching the far box. Mia held the red bus still.

"Stop a moment. A bigger push won't tell us what's wrong," Ben said.

"I wanted the first delivery to be the first try," Mia said.

"The end doesn't reach the other box. There's air under the last bit," Ben said.

"What if I fold it, like the bridge we made yesterday?" Mia asked.

"Folding won't make this piece longer," Ben said.

"Then could the wheels jump?" Mia asked.

"This is a delivery, not a flying lesson," Ben said.

"I have another strip in the tray. Could we join them?" Mia asked.

"Yes, but I don't want the join hanging over the river," Ben said.

"A block underneath the join?" Mia asked.

"Exactly. You hold the strips still; I'll put the block in place," Ben said.

Mia overlapped the two strips and taped them. Ben slid the spare block under the join.

"Now both ends have somewhere to rest," Mia said.

"And the middle has somewhere too. No road floating on wishes," Ben said.

"A slow delivery, then. No flying," Mia said.

The taped road rested on the two boxes and its new middle support.

Mia rolled the red bus across. Ben lifted the paper parcel from its roof onto the station.

"Delivered. Your driver didn't even lose a corner of the parcel," Ben said.

"What did I bring you?" Mia asked.

"A very small thank-you note. It says we make a good bridge team," Ben said.

"Then the next delivery is yours. I'll be the station," Mia said.

The little parcel rested at the station. Ben turned the red bus around while Mia opened a hand beside the other end of the bridge.

### Story QA

**Why did they need a second strip?**

The original bridge ended before reaching the far box. A second strip lengthened the road, and a block supported the place where the strips met.

**Why was tape alone not their whole plan?**

The joined strips would meet above the pretend river. They put a block directly beneath that join rather than leaving it unsupported.

**How did they check that the repair worked?**

They rolled the red bus across the repaired bridge without a running start. It reached the far box, and Ben unloaded the parcel at the station.
