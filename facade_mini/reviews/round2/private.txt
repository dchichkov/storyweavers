THE LAST PLACE SETTING

You arrive at Mara and Eli's flat for their anniversary dinner. They asked you, an old friend, to be their only guest.

Three plates wait under a low lamp. On the sideboard are a folded letter, a face-down receipt, and a photograph of the two of them outside an empty shop.

Eli takes your coat. "Perfect timing. We were just deciding what to celebrate."

Mara: "Our anniversary, Eli. That was the invitation."

> empathize mara

Mara: "I want to say something without having to defend it before I have finished."

Eli: "All right. I will wait until you have finished."

Mara pulls out the third chair. "Sit beside whoever you like. Apparently that matters tonight."

Eli: "It doesn't." He straightens the fork beside your plate without looking at her.

> talk mara

You ask Mara if you can help in the kitchen. Eli stays at the table.

Mara runs the tap until the water covers the sound of your voices. "For a minute, I need to be a person, not half of a couple."

> ask work

Mara stands beside the sink rather than facing you. "There is a restoration residency. Six months, starting Monday. I accepted it."

She watches a drop travel down the tap. "Eli doesn't know. I keep trying to find a version of the sentence that won't hurt him. There isn't one."

You can hear a chair move in the other room, but not Eli's voice.

> ask fear

Mara: "My mother used to say she would paint when things settled down. When we were older. When the spare room was clear."

"After she died, we found twenty-three untouched canvases in that room. Still in their wrapping."

She looks through the doorway. "Eli isn't my father. I know that. Sometimes I punish him for a life he hasn't asked me to live."

> promise privacy

You: "I will let you tell your partner yourself. I won't carry the conversation back for you."

Mara: "Thank you. That makes it easier to think about actually doing it."

> encourage honesty

You: "I won't say it for you. I can stay beside you while you say it."

Mara: "All right. When we go back, let me start. Even if I get it wrong."

> return

You and Mara return to the dining room. Eli looks up but does not ask you to report the conversation.

Mara unfolds the letter. "I accepted a six-month restoration residency in another city. I leave on Monday."

Eli: "Accepted. Not applied."

Mara: "Yes. I told myself I would tell you when the right evening came."

> empathize eli

Eli: "I keep reaching for a joke because I don't know what my face looks like when I am afraid."

Mara: "You can just look afraid. I know that face."

Eli notices you looking at the white scar on his thumb. "That was her bicycle. The first time I fixed it."

Mara: "You refused money."

Eli: "You bought me dinner."

> talk eli

You ask Eli if you can help in the kitchen. Mara stays at the table.

Eli busies himself with a tea towel. "I don't want to make you choose sides. I just need someone who isn't already disappointed."

> ask work

Eli closes the kitchen door most of the way. "I paid a deposit on the shop downstairs. Out of our joint account."

"I had this picture of telling her over dinner. Her laughing. Us going down with a tape measure."

He turns a mug so the chipped side faces the wall. "Mara hasn't seen the amount. I know how this sounds."

> ask fear

Eli: "When we met I could fix things for her. A lamp, a hinge, her bike. There was always a way to make myself useful."

"Lately she needs things I can't build. I keep offering the things I can."

He rubs a white scar on his thumb. "I'm afraid that if I stop being useful, there won't be a reason to keep me."

> promise privacy

You: "I will let you tell your partner yourself. I won't carry the conversation back for you."

Eli: "Thank you. That makes it easier to think about actually doing it."

> encourage honesty

You: "I won't say it for you. I can stay beside you while you say it."

Eli: "All right. When we go back, let me start. Even if I get it wrong."

> return

You and Eli return to the dining room. Mara looks up but does not ask you to report the conversation.

Eli turns over the receipt. "I paid the deposit on the shop downstairs. From our joint savings."

Mara: "You spent the money I thought we were keeping for choices."

Eli: "I thought a workshop of our own would make you want to stay."

> open floor

You move your chair a little back from the table. "I can listen. You do not need to speak through me."

Mara looks at Eli. He looks back, then down at his hands. For once, you do not fill the gap.

Mara holds up a hand before you can answer for her. "No. I need to say this myself."

"I kept the residency from you because I was afraid of being talked out of it. But I didn't give you a chance to be anything except the person I was afraid of."

Eli: "I would have been hurt. I am hurt. But I might have surprised you."

Mara: "I know. I took that away from both of us."

> listen

Eli draws the receipt toward his own plate. "This belongs on my side of the table."

"I spent our money on a future I hadn't asked you to want. I called it a gift because that made it harder for you to refuse."

Mara: "Thank you for saying it without explaining why I ought to be pleased."

Eli: "I still wanted to make you happy. That doesn't make what I did fair."

> listen

Mara looks past you to Eli. "Could we talk tomorrow without trying to win? Not about whether I am allowed to go. About what going means for us."

Eli: "And about how I return the money, or as much of it as I can. Before I make another plan."

They have stopped looking at you for permission. You move your glass out of the space between them.

> listen

They let the unfinished argument drop. There is a smaller, more immediate decision to make.

Eli: "I keep wanting to do something useful before you leave. Something bigger than words."

Mara: "Ask me for something small."

> listen

Eli: "Do you want help packing?"

Mara: "With the brushes. There are rules about how the tips are wrapped."

Eli: "Then you can teach me the rules."

> listen

Mara brings an empty brush roll to the table. She shows him where a sleeve must stay loose.

For once he waits until she has finished explaining before reaching for it.

> listen

Eli goes to fetch the soup. When he returns, the serving dish is only warm against his hands.

Eli: "I can heat it again."

Mara: "It doesn't have to be an occasion. We can just eat."

> listen

They each take a little. You do too. For a minute the sounds are ordinary: a spoon, the water jug, a chair against the floor.

No one proposes a toast.

> listen

Mara: "We are still here."

Eli: "For tonight."

She nods. It is the first answer this evening neither of them tries to enlarge.

> listen

Mara looks around the table. "We have done enough deciding for one evening."

Eli: "Yes. Now we have to live with what we have said."

> listen

The hosts let the room grow quiet. You reach toward the back of your chair.

> listen

Eli turns on the hall light. It falls across the floor between the table and the door.

Eli places the receipt beside the letter. He asks Mara whether she would like him to move his chair closer. She says yes.

Mara: "Tomorrow I want to show you the painting. Then we can talk about the parts that frighten us."

Eli: "I would like to see it." He does not offer to make a frame.

You collect your coat. As the door closes, the two papers stay between their plates, uncovered. Their chairs are on the same side of the table, with room left between them.
