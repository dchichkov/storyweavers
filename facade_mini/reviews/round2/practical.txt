THE LAST PLACE SETTING

You arrive at Mara and Eli's flat for their anniversary dinner. They asked you, an old friend, to be their only guest.

Three plates wait under a low lamp. On the sideboard are a folded letter, a face-down receipt, and a photograph of the two of them outside an empty shop.

Eli takes your coat. "Perfect timing. We were just deciding what to celebrate."

Mara: "Our anniversary, Eli. That was the invitation."

> listen

Mara pulls out the third chair. "Sit beside whoever you like. Apparently that matters tonight."

Eli: "It doesn't." He straightens the fork beside your plate without looking at her.

> ask letter

Mara: "The conservation institute wrote to me. It isn't a bill."

Eli: "You applied?"

Mara: "Yes."

> ask letter

Mara unfolds the letter. "I accepted a six-month restoration residency in another city. I leave on Monday."

Eli: "Accepted. Not applied."

Mara: "Yes. I told myself I would tell you when the right evening came."

> ask receipt

Eli: "It's a deposit. For the empty shop in the photograph."

Mara: "A deposit? Eli, how much planning have you done?"

> ask receipt

Eli turns over the receipt. "I paid the deposit on the shop downstairs. From our joint savings."

Mara: "You spent the money I thought we were keeping for choices."

Eli: "I thought a workshop of our own would make you want to stay."

> ask time

Mara: "The train is at nine-twelve on Monday. I have looked at the ticket so often I can see the numbers when I close my eyes."

Eli: "There is a ticket too."

> ask time

Mara: "There is a break after six weeks. I could come home then. We could decide together how to handle the rest."

Eli: "You imagined coming home."

Mara: "Of course I did."

> ask money

Eli: "The deposit was eight hundred pounds. Saying the number out loud makes it sound less like an idea."

Mara: "It was never just an idea to our bank account."

> ask money

Eli reads from the receipt. "Six hundred back if I call before noon tomorrow. Two hundred gone either way."

Mara: "Then there is still something you can do without deciding my future for me."

> plan refund

You: "Can you call before noon tomorrow and recover the six hundred, without making Mara responsible for the loss?"

Eli: "Yes. I made the payment. I will make the call, and I will tell the agent it is my decision."

Mara: "That is something I can believe because I will be able to see whether it happens."

Mara asks for the receipt. Eli slides it across without covering the amount.

Mara: "Eight hundred pounds."

Eli: "Yes."

> plan visit

You: "Could you put the first visit on the calendar, and agree to talk before deciding the rest?"

Mara: "Yes. The break after six weeks. I should have let Eli into the planning instead of making him find out after it."

Eli: "I can work with a date. And with being told when a date has to change."

They let the unfinished argument drop. There is a smaller, more immediate decision to make.

Eli finds a pencil and turns a spare envelope over. He draws two columns, then crosses out the line between them.

Mara: "What was that?"

Eli: "Mine and yours. We should start with what we actually have."

> listen

They write down the deposit, the recoverable amount, the train fare, and the rent.

Mara: "The two hundred pounds is real. We can be angry about it and still know what happens next."

Eli writes the call deadline at the top, larger than the amounts.

> listen

Eli: "I will make the call. I won't tell the agent that you changed your mind."

Mara: "Thank you. I hadn't changed a mind I wasn't asked for."

He leaves the pencil between their hands instead of keeping it.

> listen

Mara opens the calendar to Monday. She writes the train time in the margin, leaving the day itself mostly empty.

Eli: "You don't have to fill every square to make me feel better."

> listen

Mara marks the break after six weeks. "This is a visit, not a test I have to pass."

Eli: "And if one of us finds the distance harder than we expected, we say so before making another plan around it."

Mara draws a small question mark after the visit rather than an exclamation point.

> listen

Mara: "I should have let you into the planning. That doesn't mean asking permission to want it."

Eli: "I am learning the difference."

The open calendar takes up the place where your plate was supposed to go.

> listen

Mara looks around the table. "We have done enough deciding for one evening."

Eli: "Yes. Now we have to live with what we have said."

> listen

The hosts let the room grow quiet. You reach toward the back of your chair.

> listen

Eli turns on the hall light. It falls across the floor between the table and the door.

Eli writes "call before noon" on the envelope. Mara writes the time of the Monday train underneath and draws a line to the first six-week break.

Eli: "I am still angry."

Mara: "So am I. We can do these things while we are angry."

You leave them with the envelope between them. Nothing on it says that their relationship is fixed. It says who will make the call, when the train leaves, and when they will talk again.
Ending: practical
