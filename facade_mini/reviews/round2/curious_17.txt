THE LAST PLACE SETTING

You arrive at Mara and Eli's flat for their anniversary dinner. They asked you, an old friend, to be their only guest.

Three plates wait under a low lamp. On the sideboard are a folded letter, a face-down receipt, and a photograph of the two of them outside an empty shop.

Eli takes your coat. "Perfect timing. We were just deciding what to celebrate."

Mara: "Our anniversary, Eli. That was the invitation."

> separate

Mara: "Do not turn the next thirty years into a suggestion before we have spoken about tonight."

Eli: "That is our decision to reach. Give us time to reach it."

Mara pulls out the third chair. "Sit beside whoever you like. Apparently that matters tonight."

Eli: "It doesn't." He straightens the fork beside your plate without looking at her.

> reflect eli

Eli: "You don't know what I've done. Ask me before you judge it."

The record reaches a song Mara knows. She reaches toward the player, then stops.

Eli: "Our song."

Mara: "Your favorite song."

> open floor

You move your chair a little back from the table. "I can listen. You do not need to speak through me."

Mara looks at Eli. He looks back, then down at his hands. For once, you do not fill the gap.

Eli: "You never said you didn't like it."

Mara: "I'm saying it now. Can that be enough?"

> blame eli

Eli: "You came here for dinner. Don't tell me what my whole life means."

Mara: "I didn't ask you to attack anyone."

Eli lifts the needle. In the sudden quiet, the refrigerator sounds loud.

Eli: "All right. I can hear you."

> ask dinner

Eli: "Eleven years. The actual day was Tuesday, but we wanted a whole evening."

Mara: "We have certainly managed that."

> support eli

Eli: "At least you understand why I feel cornered."

Mara: "So I'm the problem. That was quick."

From the kitchen comes the quiet click of the oven cooling. Eli has switched it off without bringing the food through.

Mara: "You made the soup."

Eli: "Your mother's recipe."

> ask money

Mara: "We put money aside every month. It was meant to buy us choices."

Eli looks toward the receipt. "I thought I was choosing one."

> ask letter

Mara: "The conservation institute wrote to me. It isn't a bill."

Eli: "You applied?"

Mara: "Yes."

> reflect mara

Mara: "You don't know what I've done. Ask me before you judge it."

Eli looks toward the kitchen. "The soup can wait another minute."

Mara: "She never measured the cumin. You wrote down half a teaspoon and she laughed at you."

Eli: "I wanted to get it right."

Mara: "I know you did."

> plan refund

Eli: "Before you tell me what to do with the money, hear what happened and what the terms actually are."

Eli: "I can still do that. Make something you like."

Mara: "Yes. But you have to let me tell you when I like something else."

He removes the serving spoon from its wrapping and lays it between them.

> separate

Mara: "Do not turn the next thirty years into a suggestion before we have spoken about tonight."

Eli: "That is our decision to reach. Give us time to reach it."

Mara notices you looking at the letter and folds it smaller.

Eli: "More work?"

Mara: "Something like that."

> empathize eli

Eli: "I keep reaching for a joke because I don't know what my face looks like when I am afraid."

Mara: "You can just look afraid. I know that face."

Eli turns over the receipt. "I paid the deposit on the shop downstairs. From our joint savings."

Mara: "You spent the money I thought we were keeping for choices."

Eli: "I thought a workshop of our own would make you want to stay."

> listen

Eli draws the receipt toward his own plate. "This belongs on my side of the table."

"I spent our money on a future I hadn't asked you to want. I called it a gift because that made it harder for you to refuse."

Mara: "Thank you for saying it without explaining why I ought to be pleased."

Eli: "I still wanted to make you happy. That doesn't make what I did fair."

> apologize

You: "I'm sorry for what I said. You didn't invite me here to be judged."

Eli: "Thank you. Give me a little time to stop expecting another attack."

Mara looks back at the letter. "We were talking about my work."

Mara: "I need to do something that belongs to me."

Eli: "I thought we were building something together."

> plan visit

Mara: "I will not invent a date to make the room feel better. Ask me what the schedule allows."

Mara leaves the folded letter on the sideboard, within reach.

Mara: "If you want to ask about it, ask me directly."

> ask time

Mara: "There is always a reason to wait for a better week. I am beginning to think the better week is a place people send things they don't want to face."

> ask photo

Mara: "We posed outside that empty shop on our first anniversary."

Eli: "You said you wanted a studio with those windows."

Mara: "I remember. I want you to remember that I can change."

> ask time

Mara: "You should ask me about the letter before we start discussing dates."

> empathize mara

Mara: "I want to say something without having to defend it before I have finished."

Eli: "All right. I will wait until you have finished."

Mara unfolds the letter. "I accepted a six-month restoration residency in another city. I leave on Monday."

Eli: "Accepted. Not applied."

Mara: "Yes. I told myself I would tell you when the right evening came."

> ask future

Eli: "I used to think the future meant more of this. A little less rent. Better chairs."

Mara: "I think I need it to include something neither of us has seen yet."

> support mara

Mara: "At least you understand why I feel cornered."

Eli: "So I'm the problem. That was quick."

Mara holds up a hand before you can answer for her. "No. I need to say this myself."

"I kept the residency from you because I was afraid of being talked out of it. But I didn't give you a chance to be anything except the person I was afraid of."

Eli: "I would have been hurt. I am hurt. But I might have surprised you."

Mara: "I know. I took that away from both of us."

> mediate

You: "Tomorrow, could you talk about both decisions before making another one?"

Mara: "A conversation. Not a promise that I stay."

Eli: "Yes. I can agree to that."

Eli: "I keep wanting to do something useful before you leave. Something bigger than words."

Mara: "Ask me for something small."

> ask money

Eli reads from the receipt. "Six hundred back if I call before noon tomorrow. Two hundred gone either way."

Mara: "Then there is still something you can do without deciding my future for me."

> ask family

Mara follows your glance toward the painting above the radiator. "My mother did that one. Before she had children."

Eli: "Mara inherited her eye."

Mara: "I am trying not to inherit all her postponements."

> reflect eli

Eli: "I have said what I did. I need to show what I do differently now."

Eli: "You were telling me what help would actually be useful."

Eli: "Do you want help packing?"

Mara: "With the brushes. There are rules about how the tips are wrapped."

Eli: "Then you can teach me the rules."

> separate

Mara: "Do not turn the next thirty years into a suggestion before we have spoken about tonight."

Eli: "That is our decision to reach. Give us time to reach it."

Mara brings an empty brush roll to the table. She shows him where a sleeve must stay loose.

For once he waits until she has finished explaining before reaching for it.

> ask work

Eli: "I mend things. Doors, bikes, whatever people bring. Mara works on things you can ruin by trying too hard to fix them."

Mara: "That is a better description than you usually give."

He smiles briefly. "I do listen sometimes."

> listen

Eli goes to fetch the soup. When he returns, the serving dish is only warm against his hands.

Eli: "I can heat it again."

Mara: "It doesn't have to be an occasion. We can just eat."

> listen

They each take a little. You do too. For a minute the sounds are ordinary: a spoon, the water jug, a chair against the floor.

No one proposes a toast.

> ask future

Mara: "Being frightened of losing each other is not the same as wanting the same life."

Eli: "And being angry tonight is not the same as never wanting it again."

Neither asks you to settle that for them.

> separate

Mara: "Do not turn the next thirty years into a suggestion before we have spoken about tonight."

Eli: "That is our decision to reach. Give us time to reach it."

Eli gestures to the serving dish. "Please. Have some while there is still warmth in it."

Mara: "We are still here."

Eli: "For tonight."

She nods. It is the first answer this evening neither of them tries to enlarge.

> listen

Mara looks around the table. "We have done enough deciding for one evening."

Eli: "Yes. Now we have to live with what we have said."

> mediate

Eli: "We heard you. Give us a moment to do something with it."

The hosts let the room grow quiet. You reach toward the back of your chair.

> ask letter

Mara: "The acceptance has my name on it. The dates are there in writing."

> talk mara

You ask Mara if you can help in the kitchen. Eli stays at the table.

Mara runs the tap until the water covers the sound of your voices. "For a minute, I need to be a person, not half of a couple."

> apologize

Mara: "You don't need to apologize for being here."

> listen

You leave the silence unfilled. Mara dries a glass and sets it carefully on the counter.

> listen

For a while you both listen to the kitchen clock. Neither of you reaches for another task.

> apologize

Mara: "You don't need to apologize for being here."

> listen

Mara: "We should go back. I don't want silence in here to become another thing we hide behind."

You and Mara return to the dining room. Eli looks up but does not ask you to report the conversation.

Eli checks the time. "It is getting late."

Eli turns on the hall light. It falls across the floor between the table and the door.

Eli places the receipt beside the letter. He asks Mara whether she would like him to move his chair closer. She says yes.

Mara: "Tomorrow I want to show you the painting. Then we can talk about the parts that frighten us."

Eli: "I would like to see it." He does not offer to make a frame.

You collect your coat. As the door closes, the two papers stay between their plates, uncovered. Their chairs are on the same side of the table, with room left between them.
