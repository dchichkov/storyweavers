THE LAST PLACE SETTING

You arrive at Mara and Eli's flat for their anniversary dinner. They asked you, an old friend, to be their only guest.

Three plates wait under a low lamp. On the sideboard are a folded letter, a face-down receipt, and a photograph of the two of them outside an empty shop.

Eli takes your coat. "Perfect timing. We were just deciding what to celebrate."

Mara: "Our anniversary, Eli. That was the invitation."

> plan refund

Eli: "Before you tell me what to do with the money, hear what happened and what the terms actually are."

Mara pulls out the third chair. "Sit beside whoever you like. Apparently that matters tonight."

Eli: "It doesn't." He straightens the fork beside your plate without looking at her.

> separate

Mara: "Do not turn the next thirty years into a suggestion before we have spoken about tonight."

Eli: "That is our decision to reach. Give us time to reach it."

Eli notices you looking at the white scar on his thumb. "That was her bicycle. The first time I fixed it."

Mara: "You refused money."

Eli: "You bought me dinner."

> ask receipt

Eli: "It's a deposit. For the empty shop in the photograph."

Mara: "A deposit? Eli, how much planning have you done?"

> empathize eli

Eli: "I keep reaching for a joke because I don't know what my face looks like when I am afraid."

Mara: "You can just look afraid. I know that face."

Eli looks at the scar on his thumb. "I keep trying to fix things."

Mara: "I would have bought you dinner if you had made the bike worse."

Eli: "I didn't know that."

Mara: "You were funny. I liked you. The bike was a bike."

> listen

Eli rests his hands flat on the table. "I think I am better at being needed than being wanted."

Mara: "Being wanted involves letting me choose."

He turns his hands palm up, as if he has finally put down a tool.

> listen

Eli moves the receipt away from a ring of spilled water.

Mara: "What did you buy?"

Eli: "An opportunity."

> plan visit

Mara: "I will not invent a date to make the room feel better. Ask me what the schedule allows."

Eli: "I wanted to give you somewhere to work."

Mara: "Then you could have asked where I wanted to work."

> reflect eli

Eli: "You don't know what I've done. Ask me before you judge it."

Eli leaves the receipt on the sideboard. His thumb has creased one corner.

Eli: "It was meant to be good news."

> listen

Mara: "We could eat before everything gets cold."

Eli: "I thought we might have a toast first."

> space

You: "Would a night apart give you room to think?"

Mara: "I can stay with my sister. One night, for now."

Eli: "I won't try to stop you."

They let the unfinished argument drop. There is a smaller, more immediate decision to make.

Mara sends her sister a message asking whether the spare room is free. She writes "one night" and does not erase it.

Eli watches the tabletop instead of the screen.

> support mara

Mara: "At least you understand why I feel cornered."

Eli: "So I'm the problem. That was quick."

The answer comes back: Of course. Mara reads those two words aloud.

Eli: "Do you need the bigger bag?"

Mara: "The small one. Please don't make it mean more than I said."

> listen

Eli: "Keep your key."

Mara: "I was going to."

He nods. The exchange is awkward, but neither turns it into a threat.

> ask time

Mara: "There is always a reason to wait for a better week. I am beginning to think the better week is a place people send things they don't want to face."

> blame eli

Eli: "You came here for dinner. Don't tell me what my whole life means."

Mara: "I didn't ask you to attack anyone."

Eli goes to fetch the soup. When he returns, the serving dish is only warm against his hands.

Eli: "I can heat it again."

Mara: "It doesn't have to be an occasion. We can just eat."

> ask time

Mara: "You should ask me about the letter before we start discussing dates."

> ask art

Mara: "I restored part of a portrait for a test. Just one eye. If you move too fast, what you think is dirt turns out to be somebody's shadow."

Eli: "You never showed me."

Mara: "You never asked."

> empathize eli

Eli: "We heard you. Give us a moment to do something with it."

Eli gestures to the serving dish. "Please. Have some while there is still warmth in it."

They each take a little. You do too. For a minute the sounds are ordinary: a spoon, the water jug, a chair against the floor.

No one proposes a toast.

> ask future

Eli: "I used to think the future meant more of this. A little less rent. Better chairs."

Mara: "I think I need it to include something neither of us has seen yet."

> reflect eli

Eli: "You don't know what I've done. Ask me before you judge it."

Eli gestures to the serving dish. "Please. Have some while there is still warmth in it."

Mara: "We are still here."

Eli: "For tonight."

She nods. It is the first answer this evening neither of them tries to enlarge.

> ask art

Mara: "The director wanted to know why I stopped. He valued that I could tell where my certainty ended."

Eli: "Is that what you wanted from me? Interest, without a solution?"

Mara: "That would have been a start."

> ask family

Mara follows your glance toward the painting above the radiator. "My mother did that one. Before she had children."

Eli: "Mara inherited her eye."

Mara: "I am trying not to inherit all her postponements."

> mediate

Mara: "Don't ask us to make a promise before we've owned what happened."

Mara looks around the table. "We have done enough deciding for one evening."

Eli: "Yes. Now we have to live with what we have said."

> listen

Mara feels for her flat key in her pocket. "I am keeping this."

Eli: "Of course."

> ask art

Mara opens a photograph of the painting on her phone. Eli leans closer, then waits for her to point.

She shows him where the varnish ends. He asks a question about the light.

For a few sentences, she speaks to him in the voice she uses when she loves her work.

> space

Eli: "We heard you. Give us a moment to do something with it."

Eli checks the time. "It is getting late."

Eli turns on the hall light. It falls across the floor between the table and the door.

Mara checks that her flat key is in her pocket, packs the small bag, and joins you in the hall.

Eli: "Tomorrow?"

Mara: "Tomorrow. We do not have to decide what every tomorrow means tonight."

He holds the door without standing in it. On the stairs she pauses to settle the bag on her shoulder. Her key rings softly against the buckle; she has not given it back.
Ending: space
