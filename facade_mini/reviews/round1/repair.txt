THE LAST PLACE SETTING

You arrive at Mara and Eli's flat for their anniversary dinner. They asked you, an old friend, to be their only guest.

Three plates wait under a low lamp. On the sideboard are a folded letter, a face-down receipt, and a photograph of the two of them outside an empty shop.

Eli takes your coat. "Perfect timing. We were just deciding what to celebrate."

Mara: "Our anniversary, Eli. That was the invitation."

> empathize mara

Mara: "I want to say something without having to defend it before I have finished."

Eli: "All right. I will wait until you have finished."

Mara pulls out the third chair. "Sit beside whoever you like. Apparently that matters tonight."

Eli: "It doesn't." He straightens the fork beside your plate without looking at her.

> ask letter

Mara: "The conservation institute wrote to me. It isn't a bill."

Eli: "You applied?"

Mara: "Yes."

> ask letter

Mara unfolds the letter. "I accepted a six-month restoration residency in another city. I leave on Monday."

Eli: "Accepted. Not applied."

Mara: "Yes. I told myself I would tell you when the right evening came."

> empathize eli

Eli: "I keep reaching for a joke because I don't know what my face looks like when I am afraid."

Mara: "You can just look afraid. I know that face."

Eli looks at the open letter. "Monday. You had a date all this time."

> ask receipt

Eli: "It's a deposit. For the empty shop in the photograph."

Mara: "A deposit? Eli, how much planning have you done?"

> ask receipt

Eli turns over the receipt. "I paid the deposit on the shop downstairs. From our joint savings."

Mara: "You spent the money I thought we were keeping for choices."

Eli: "I thought a workshop of our own would make you want to stay."

> reflect mara

Mara: "I chose not to tell you about the residency. Being afraid of the argument doesn't excuse that."

Eli: "That is the first time I've heard you say it without a but."

Mara looks back at the letter. "We were talking about my work."

Mara: "I still want to go. I want to be able to say that to you honestly."

Eli: "I am trying to hear the difference."

> reflect eli

Eli: "I used our money without asking. Calling it a gift doesn't make that fair."

Mara: "That is the first time I've heard you say it without a but."

Mara leaves the letter where Eli can read it. "At least it isn't hidden now."

> mediate

You: "Tomorrow, could you talk about both decisions before making another one?"

Mara: "A conversation. Not a promise that I stay."

Eli: "Yes. I can agree to that."

Eli: "I keep wanting to do something useful before you leave. Something bigger than words."

Mara: "Ask me for something small."

> listen

Eli: "Do you want help packing?"

Mara: "With the brushes. There are rules about how the tips are wrapped."

Eli: "Then you can teach me the rules."

> listen

Mara brings an empty brush roll to the table. She shows him where a sleeve must stay loose.

For once he waits until she has finished explaining before reaching for it.

> listen

Eli goes to fetch the soup. When he returns, the serving dish is only warm against his hands.

Eli: "I can heat it again."

Mara: "It doesn't have to be an occasion. We can just eat."

> listen

They each take a little. You do too. For a minute the sounds are ordinary: a spoon, the water jug, a chair against the floor.

No one proposes a toast.

> listen

Mara: "We are still here."

Eli: "For tonight."

She nods. It is the first answer this evening neither of them tries to enlarge.

> listen

Eli: "We can't leave tonight exactly where we started."

Mara: "Then let's choose one thing we can actually do. Not a whole life."

> listen

Mara and Eli wait. For once, neither fills the silence for the other.

> listen

Eli lowers the hall light. The evening has come to its last decision.

Eli places the receipt beside the letter. He asks Mara whether she would like him to move his chair closer. She says yes.

Mara: "Tomorrow I want to show you the painting. Then we can talk about the parts that frighten us."

Eli: "I would like to see it." He does not offer to make a frame.

You collect your coat. As the door closes, the two papers stay between their plates, uncovered. Their chairs are on the same side of the table, with room left between them.
