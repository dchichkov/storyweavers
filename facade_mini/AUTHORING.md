# Authoring a compatible addition

The central production cost is not defining a new beat name. It is making its
entry, dramatic turn, interruptions, alternatives, and consequences work in the
situations where the director can actually choose it.

## Scene contract

Before adding a scene, write down:

1. What each character wants in this exchange, and what observable choice changes.
2. Which people are present, which facts each already knows, and which props exist.
3. What has to be true for entry; what would make the scene redundant or false.
4. The change made by each performance, including the audience of new knowledge.
5. How the scene resumes if another topic interrupts it before its key information.
6. What the later narrative can observe as proof that this scene mattered.

Use a new `Beat` for a sustained exchange with its own turn. Use a mix-in for a
short interruption that can rejoin several scenes. Use a social exchange for a
reusable player intervention with state-dependent willingness. Use a private
performance when knowledge genuinely belongs to a restricted audience.

## Concrete examples in this library

`refund_window` requires a public deposit disclosure. Its terms can also be
learned by asking about money. If the guest has already learned the terms, the
scene is redundant and cannot be selected. If the terms are learned during an
interruption after the scene has started, the pending goal uses a reminder.

`calendar_margin` requires Mara's accepted visit commitment. It moves the calendar
into the room and records the agreed dates. The practical ending reads the two
commitments; it cannot be obtained by a generic friendly gesture.

The kitchen disclosures update only the guest's knowledge. Returning does not
broadcast them. The host may choose to disclose after agreeing to do so; the guest
may also announce the confidence, with an explicit trust violation. The two paths
share factual content but have different causes and consequences.

## Compatibility review

Read the addition in context, not only alone. At minimum exercise it after:

- A direct public disclosure and a private disclosure.
- The same information arriving earlier through another scene.
- A fresh accusation, an apology for that accusation, and a repeated apology.
- An interruption immediately before its central goal.
- A different agreement already being reached.
- A late change during the closing phase.

Check subject and audience knowledge. A character who was absent must not act
surprised by information they supposedly heard earlier, nor answer a private
question from another room. Avoid creating new motives solely to support a
convenient ending. A practical commitment is not the same as forgiveness.

Test the explicit world effects and save full transcripts. Read the end image
against the actual prop locations and states. Do not repair contradictions by
rewriting rendered English after execution.

## Reproducible extension trial

For an actual authoring-cost comparison, use the same brief in both conditions:
add a fourth physical clue tied to a distinct disagreement, three compatible
mix-ins, one sustained scene, a private interpretation, and an observable effect
on an existing ending. Freeze the engine first. Record authoring time, review
time, rejected versions, and any shared infrastructure changes separately.

Predeclare acceptance sequences and reserve others for an independent reviewer.
Have readers judge complete conversations without knowing which condition
produced them. Report coherence and agency defects alongside time. Do not infer
savings from token counts, source lines, or the number of shuffled scene paths.
