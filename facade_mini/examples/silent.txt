THE LAST PLACE SETTING

You arrive at Mara and Eli's flat for their anniversary dinner. They asked you, an old friend, to be their only guest.

Three plates wait under a low lamp. On the sideboard are a folded letter, a face-down receipt, and a photograph of the two of them outside an empty shop.

Eli takes your coat. "Perfect timing. We were just deciding what to celebrate."

Mara: "Our anniversary, Eli. That was the invitation."

> listen

Mara pulls out the third chair. "Sit beside whoever you like. Apparently that matters tonight."

Eli: "It doesn't." He straightens the fork beside your plate without looking at her.

> listen

Eli notices you looking at the white scar on his thumb. "That was her bicycle. The first time I fixed it."

Mara: "You refused money."

Eli: "You bought me dinner."

> listen

Mara: "I would have bought you dinner if you had made the bike worse."

Eli: "I didn't know that."

Mara: "You were funny. I liked you. The bike was a bike."

> listen

Eli rests his hands flat on the table. "I think I am better at being needed than being wanted."

Mara: "Being wanted involves letting me choose."

He turns his hands palm up, as if he has finally put down a tool.

> listen

Eli turns the photograph toward you. "Remember this place? We said we could make anything there."

Mara: "We were twenty-three. We said a lot of things."

> listen

Eli: "You used to like that picture."

Mara: "I still do. I don't want to live inside it."

> listen

Eli sets the photograph down carefully, leaving it facing Mara.

Mara: "Ask me what I want now. Not what I wanted when that was taken."

> listen

Mara: "We could eat before everything gets cold."

Eli: "I thought we might have a toast first."

> listen

Mara: "Did you invite our friend for dinner, or for an audience?"

Eli: "I thought we behaved better with someone here."

> listen

Mara looks at you. "You don't have to decide who wins."

Eli draws his glass back from the center of the table.

> listen

Mara places her hand over the letter. "There is something I should say. I don't feel safe saying it like this."

> listen

Eli covers the receipt with his palm. "I had a plan. I can hear how it would sound now."

> listen

Mara: "We keep making decisions around each other."

Eli looks at you. "How do we stop doing that?"

> listen

Eli goes to fetch the soup. When he returns, the serving dish is only warm against his hands.

Eli: "I can heat it again."

Mara: "It doesn't have to be an occasion. We can just eat."

> listen

They each take a little. You do too. For a minute the sounds are ordinary: a spoon, the water jug, a chair against the floor.

No one proposes a toast.

> listen

Mara: "We are still here."

Eli: "For tonight."

She nods. It is the first answer this evening neither of them tries to enlarge.

> listen

Mara looks around the table. "I think we have reached the end of what we can say tonight."

Eli: "Then we should not keep talking just to avoid saying good night."

> listen

The hosts let the room grow quiet. You reach toward the back of your chair.

> listen

Eli turns on the hall light. It falls across the floor between the table and the door.

Eli: "We should do this again when things are less complicated."

Mara opens the door. "Good night."

You take your coat. The letter stays folded and the receipt stays face down. Behind the courtesy, the two small papers continue to occupy the room.
